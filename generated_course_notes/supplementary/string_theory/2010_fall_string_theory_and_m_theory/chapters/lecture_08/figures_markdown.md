# Figure Notes

## Blackboard Descriptions

### Figure 1: Two-Dimensional Electrostatics

The left board contains a vertical progression from the divergence equation
to Poisson's equation and then to the explicit two-dimensional Laplacian.
The visible plus sign between the \(x\)- and \(y\)-derivatives is the point
used immediately afterward to distinguish this static problem from a wave
equation.

### Figure 2: Cauchy--Riemann Equations

The completed board pair reads \(u_x=v_y\) and \(u_y=-v_x\). The second
line's minus sign is unobstructed and controls the later cancellation of
mixed derivatives.

### Figure 3: Harmonic Components

The board differentiates the Cauchy--Riemann equations and adds the results.
The mixed partials cancel, leaving the Laplace equation for one component.
The same argument for the other component is supplied in the typeset text.

### Figure 4: Angle Preservation

Two small directions in the \(z\)-plane and their images in the \(w\)-plane
are connected by the common derivative \(w'(z)\). The board ratio is
legible enough to support the proof, while the adjacent TikZ reconstruction
makes the relative-angle statement visually immediate.

### Figures 5--7: Analytic Examples

Figure 5 shows the complete \(z^2\) expansion and identifies \(u\) and \(v\).
Figure 6 gives complex conjugation as the nonanalytic contrast. Figure 7
shows the decomposition of \(e^{x+iy}\) into its real and imaginary parts.
Together these frames preserve the lecture's progression from example to
counterexample to globally useful map.

### Figures 8--11: Global Conformal Maps

Figure 8 shows rays and semicircles in the half-plane becoming a rectangular
grid on a strip under \(\log z\). Figure 9 adds the periodic identification
that turns the full-plane image into a cylinder. Figure 10 records the
linear-fractional map and unit circle. Figure 11 shows the final orthogonal
circle-and-line grid inside the disc.

The formula visible in Figure 10 is the reciprocal of the convention needed
to send the right half-plane to the disc interior. The chapter preserves the
frame and states the convention issue explicitly.

### Figures 12--13: Euclidean Worldsheet

Figure 12 captures the completed Euclidean action after both derivative
terms have acquired positive signs. Figure 13 places the slit-strip
worldsheet beside its disc representation with four boundary points. The
drawings describe parameter space, not the trajectory angles of particles
in spacetime.

### Figures 14--15: Boundary Insertions

Figure 14 separates the bulk Euclidean exponential from a momentum
insertion. Figure 15 completes the product of factors associated with all
external strings. The indexed notation and Lorentz contraction are
normalized in the TeX while retaining the board's product structure.

### Figure 16: Electrostatic Interpretation

The disc carries marked boundary sources while the board discussion assigns
one charge type to each target-space momentum component. This is evidence
for the Gaussian-integral interpretation: each \(X^\mu\) solves an
independent two-dimensional potential problem.

### Figure 17: Boundary Moduli

Four points lie around the disc boundary. Conformal freedom places three at
chosen positions, leaving only the fourth to vary. This frame anchors the
\(n-3\) tree-level boundary-moduli count.

  with a labeled comparison to the more common convention.

## Caption Policy

Captions state what is actually visible and why the frame matters. They do
not identify worldsheet coordinates with physical scattering angles, claim
unwritten normalizations are visible, or hide the interior/exterior
convention issue. Every caption is paired with its lecture timestamp.

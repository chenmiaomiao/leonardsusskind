# Narrative Map

## Central Question

How does the sum over particle worldlines become a sum over string
worldsheets, and why does conformal symmetry make one four-string surface
encode both direct and crossed scattering channels?

## Lecture Rhythm

1. Begin with the familiar free-particle action, first in nonrelativistic
   coordinates and then in a relativistic worldline parameterization.
2. Replace classical minimization by Feynman's sum over every path and show
   how joined worldline segments lead to propagators and Feynman diagrams.
3. Expose the convergence problem: every Lorentzian history has unit
   magnitude, however violently it fluctuates.
4. Rotate the path parameter into imaginary time.  The phase becomes a
   decaying Euclidean weight, wild paths are suppressed, and the answer is
   later continued back to Lorentzian kinematics.
5. Promote a particle worldline to an open-string ribbon or closed-string
   tube, described by embedding fields \(X^\mu(\sigma,\tau)\).
6. Sum over worldsheets and topologies, then Wick-rotate the worldsheet
   action so both derivative terms enter positively.
7. Compare the quadratic form with the proper-area description and proceed
   to the Euclidean equation of motion.
8. Derive Laplace's equation and discretize it until the central value is
   visibly the average of its four neighbors.
9. Use that mean-value property to motivate conformal coordinates: local
   angles and shapes survive even though scale may vary.
10. Map the four-string slit strip to a disc.  The four external strings
    become four ordered boundary insertions, with one real modulus left after
    conformal redundancy is removed.
11. Integrate that modulus to recover the beta-function structure.  Its two
    endpoint limits describe long propagation in two apparently different
    channels, revealing channel duality.

## Expository Commitments

- Preserve the distinction between an ordinary integral along a path and
  the functional integral over paths.
- Keep Wick rotation as an analytic-continuation procedure, not a literal
  claim that physical time is imaginary.
- Move from the Lorentzian wave equation to the Euclidean Laplace equation
  before invoking conformal invariance.
- Retain the finite-difference argument because it supplies the lecture's
  geometric intuition for harmonic functions.
- Keep all recoverable questions beside the argument they test.
- Preserve the original slit-strip-to-disc route before presenting the
  direct/crossed channel interpretation.

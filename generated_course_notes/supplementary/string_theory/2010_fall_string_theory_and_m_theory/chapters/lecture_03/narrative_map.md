# Narrative Map

## Opening Setup

The lecture begins by finishing a calculation rather than announcing a new
subject. The normal modes are already known; the immediate task is to put
each one into canonical oscillator form. That algebra is not an isolated
review. It creates the operators that will later expose the first open-string
particle states.

## Beat Sequence

1. **Normalize one string oscillator.** The one-quarter kinetic normalization
   leads to \(p_n=\dot X_n/2\), a Hamiltonian
   \(p_n^2+n^2X_n^2/4\), and ladder operators normalized by \(\sqrt n\).
2. **Reassemble the quantum string.** Two transverse oscillator towers are
   substituted into the Neumann cosine expansion. Audience questions test
   the dependence on \(\sigma\), \(\tau\), and the raising/lowering signs.
3. **Pause for spin.** Massive \(2J+1\) multiplets are contrasted with
   massless helicity states. Photon linear and circular polarization make the
   distinction concrete; the graviton analogy raises the amplitude-square
   question.
4. **Read the first open-string levels.** The ground state is assigned an
   unknown \(m_0^2\). Its two cheapest excitations transform as a transverse
   vector. Because there is no longitudinal third state, relativistic
   consistency makes the level massless and fixes \(m_0^2=-1\).
5. **Turn the tachyon puzzle into a stability calculation.** A formal
   superluminal group velocity is introduced and then superseded by the
   Klein--Gordon field analysis. Negative mass squared means imaginary
   low-momentum frequencies and exponential growth.
6. **Use the pendulum chain to restore causality.** The upright configuration
   is unstable, yet the falling front travels at the finite signal speed of
   the coupled system.
7. **Introduce interaction by one local rule.** Open-string endpoints join
   with amplitude \(g_s\), and time reversal gives splitting. The distinction
   between amplitude and probability is made explicit.
8. **Let an open string meet itself.** Self-joining creates a closed loop, so
   interacting open strings cannot avoid closed-string states.
9. **Move from closed strings to gravity.** Closed strings fuse without
   opening, their lowest excitation is spin two, and mixed open--closed
   interactions make graviton exchange universal.
10. **Close on deferred structure.** Questions about the string scale,
    endpoint labels, junctions, dimensional dependence, and inertia set the
    agenda for later lectures without pretending those topics were derived
    here.

## Transition Cues

- The algebra repeatedly returns to its physical payoff: the purpose of each
  normalization is to count states.
- The spin interlude is announced as a necessary preliminary, then harvested
  immediately in the first-level consistency argument.
- The word tachyon creates a deliberate false lead toward speed; the lecture
  then asks what it really means and pivots to field stability.
- Interaction begins qualitatively: no detailed amplitude is computed, but
  every later topology is built from the same joining/splitting rule.
- Audience questions dominate the final third and progressively widen the
  scope from one interaction to closed strings, gravity, junctions, units,
  and initial conditions.

## Pacing Risks

- Compressing the oscillator derivation to a standard textbook formula loses
  the factor-of-\(n\) commutator that controls the string spectrum.
- Treating massless helicity as a short definition loses the rest-frame
  argument and the photon-polarization bridge.
- Calling the tachyon merely faster than light reverses the lecture's final
  conclusion. The unstable-vacuum derivation and pendulum analogy must remain
  central.
- Calling the first open-string state the physical photon overstates the
  lecture. It is photon-like or gauge-boson-like until a specific model is
  chosen.
- The joining diagrams should remain qualitative. Detailed worldsheet
  amplitudes, brane boundary conditions, and the closed-string spectrum are
  promises for later, not hidden derivations in this chapter.

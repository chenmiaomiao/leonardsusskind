# Narrative Map

## Opening Logic

The review begins with the practical \(c=1\) convention, then returns to Galilean relativity so that the Lorentz correction has a concrete baseline. Equations are repeatedly translated into spacetime pictures and measurable operations.

## Lecture Sequence

1. Establish the Galilean transformation, universal time, the train-and-station grid, and inverse reciprocity.
2. Replace it with the Lorentz transformation, restore \(c\), recover the low-velocity limit, and note the singular \(v=c\) boundary.
3. Use transverse meter sticks and a symmetric intermediate frame to justify \(y'=y\) and \(z'=z\).
4. Rewrite the boost in \(t+x\) and \(t-x\), showing a reciprocal squeeze that preserves the light cone.
5. Return to the meter-stick objection and make invariant event coincidence operational with endpoint flashes.
6. Postpone velocity composition and turn to the quantity preserved by Lorentz transformations.
7. Derive the invariant interval by analogy with Euclidean rotations, then define and classify proper time.
8. Identify an ideal moving clock's reading with proper time and distinguish it from coordinate time.
9. Derive time dilation and resolve reciprocal clock slowing through relativity of simultaneity.
10. Compare the straight and broken twin paths, then address acceleration, ideal clocks, and integration along a curved trajectory.
11. Use exchanged light pulses to describe what the twins see, while postponing the separate energy question.
12. Preserve the compact-space question as an explicitly unresolved issue of global identification.
13. Define length on a simultaneity slice and derive longitudinal length contraction from the spacelike interval.
14. Close with questions about gravity's propagation speed, rapid interstellar travel, acceleration, and spacetime as a unified geometry.

## Characteristic Rhythm

- Familiar Newtonian structure is stated before its relativistic correction.
- Algebra is interpreted geometrically rather than left as a coordinate rule.
- Clock networks, wristwatches, flashes, meter sticks, and pulses keep invariant quantities operational.
- Apparent paradoxes are resolved by identifying the events and paths actually being compared.
- Questions requiring later machinery are postponed rather than given speculative answers.

## Fidelity Risks

- Do not omit the transverse-stick argument, invariant-event assumption, or the two follow-up questions.
- Preserve the rotation analogy and the wristwatch interpretation before presenting proper time as a polished definition.
- Keep the simultaneity resolution of reciprocal time dilation and the physical ideal-clock assumption.
- Do not convert the deferred energy question or compact-space question into settled claims.
- Retain the late length-contraction derivation after the exploratory detours, matching the recording's order.

# Narrative Map

## Lecture Arc

The lecture begins with a physical demand: the law for a charged particle must have the same form in every inertial frame. Newtonian \(F=m a\) cannot simply be carried over because relativistic observers do not share Newtonian kinematics. The first repair is to organize force as \(d\boldsymbol p/dt\), with relativity entering through the momentum.

The magnetic term then forces a deliberate mathematical detour. The cross product is rebuilt as an antisymmetric spatial tensor, including the component count, a temporary sign error, its correction, and the geometry of a plane represented by a normal arrow. The same language turns the magnetic force into \(v_nB_{nm}\) and makes the no-work theorem a direct consequence of antisymmetry.

That theorem leads to the central thought experiment. A charge moving across a magnetic field accelerates in the laboratory, while an instantaneous co-moving observer sees zero particle velocity. The magnetic term then vanishes in that frame, so an electric field must account for the same force. Electric and magnetic fields must therefore be components of one object that mixes under boosts.

The six field components are assembled into \(F^{\mu\nu}\). On the particle side, four-velocity, four-momentum, and four-force are introduced. Differentiating \(u_\mu u^\mu=1\) gives \(f^\mu u_\mu=0\), and the earlier antisymmetry argument suggests
\[
  \frac{dp^\mu}{d\tau}=qF^{\mu\nu}u_\nu.
\]
The lecture checks this first at low velocity and then exactly, showing that the common gamma factor cancels while \(\boldsymbol p=m\gamma\boldsymbol v\) retains the relativistic correction.

The final movement asks how \(F^{\mu\nu}\) transforms. A product of two vectors supplies the two-index transformation rule; the parallel field component remains fixed, while a transverse electric component mixes with a magnetic component. Questions about a current-carrying wire and the four-potential close the particle discussion.

The lecture then changes scale from a world line to fields over spacetime. The derivative of a scalar is a covector, every further derivative contributes another lower index, and contracting one raised with one lowered derivative gives the invariant scalar wave equation. The chapter ends where the lecture ends: with this equation as preparation for a covariant treatment of Maxwell's equations.

## Rhythm to Preserve

1. Begin from frame consistency, not from a catalog of formulas.
2. Let the three-dimensional tensor argument earn the four-dimensional construction.
3. Preserve the provisional sign and its correction rather than presenting the final matrix without motivation.
4. Keep the magnetic no-work proof in index form; its purpose is generalization.
5. Treat the co-moving observer as the physical reason for electric-magnetic mixing.
6. Verify the covariant force law in two passes: low velocity, then exact gamma cancellation.
7. Keep the mathematics-versus-physics exchange attached to the force derivation.
8. Present field transformations as a worked bridge, not a complete table.
9. End with the scalar wave equation and the announced Maxwell problem, without adding a detached summary.

# Narrative Map

## Lecture Arc

The lecture moves from a kinematical description of expansion to the first dynamical equations for the scale factor. It deliberately rebuilds the flat expanding metric, physical distance, Hubble's law, and null propagation before invoking Newton's shell theorem. The zero-energy projectile calculation then yields the flat Friedmann relation. The rest of the lecture changes only the energy content: nonrelativistic matter, radiation, and vacuum energy give three distinct histories for \(a(t)\). The closing questions reinterpret those results through last scattering, redshift, pressure work, time coordinates, curvature, and cosmic fate.

## Beat Sequence

1. Re-establish spatial flatness, Minkowski spacetime, and metric coefficients as gravitational fields.
2. Motivate homogeneity and isotropy observationally rather than as a theorem.
3. Use football-field surveying to explain why measured flatness only bounds curvature.
4. Introduce the flat expanding metric and the two dimensional conventions for \(a\) and \(x\).
5. Distinguish spheres from balls and build \(S^1\), \(S^2\), and \(S^3\).
6. Explain intrinsic geometry without requiring an embedding space.
7. Show why a closed expanding universe has no preferred Big Bang center.
8. Define comoving coordinates and physical distance \(D=a\Delta x\).
9. Re-derive \(v=(\dot a/a)D\) and identify \(H=\dot a/a\).
10. Use a question about lookback time to insist that \(H\) changes with epoch.
11. Derive \(dt=a\,dx\) and \(dx/dt=1/a\) from the null condition.
12. Separate coordinate light speed from local physical light speed.
13. Test uniform local physics through dimensionless atomic and nuclear comparisons.
14. Complete the positive, flat, and negative curvature comparison.
15. Center a large sphere on the observer and invoke shell cancellation.
16. Replace the enclosed homogeneous mass by an equivalent central mass.
17. Define \(R=xa\), \(v=x\dot a\), and the Newtonian projectile energy.
18. Choose zero energy and interpret it as the escape-velocity boundary.
19. Distinguish fixed enclosed mass from falling physical density.
20. Substitute \(M=(4\pi/3)\rho R^3\), use \(v=HR\), and cancel \(R^2\).
21. Obtain \(H^2=(8\pi G/3)\rho\) and compare both sides observationally.
22. Fill a comoving box with nonrelativistic matter and derive \(\rho_{\rm m}\propto a^{-3}\).
23. Normalize the density to its present value and discuss its rough magnitude.
24. Solve the matter equation by a power-law trial and then verify it by integration.
25. Preserve the live algebra correction while displaying \(a\propto t^{2/3}\) cleanly.
26. Derive \(H=2/(3t)\) and return to the lookback-time prediction.
27. Introduce the Einstein-level link between energy sign and curvature sign.
28. Replace matter by photons and use \(E=h\nu=hc/\lambda\).
29. Use wavelength stretching and the guitar-string analogy to get \(\rho_{\rm r}\propto a^{-4}\).
30. Derive the radiation history \(a\propto t^{1/2}\) and the early radiation era.
31. Define vacuum energy by constant density and explain why it dominates late.
32. Solve \(\dot a/a=H_\Lambda\) to obtain exponential expansion.
33. Identify vacuum energy with dark energy in the constant-density model.
34. Answer why photons respond to physical stretching rather than coordinate labels.
35. Build the observer-centered surface-of-last-scattering picture.
36. Relate the stretching and Doppler descriptions of cosmological redshift.
37. Use the redshift-energy question to return to expansion bookkeeping.
38. Use the movable piston to make pressure work concrete.
39. Qualify the global-energy analogy with local stress-energy conservation.
40. Remove a temporal conformal factor by redefining cosmic time.
41. Separate present expansion from eventual recollapse in a curved universe.
42. Include vacuum energy in the long-term fate and end with photon number versus photon energy.

## Transition Cues

- Observation precedes formalism: flatness, component fractions, and acceleration enter as measured facts.
- Every coordinate formula is translated back into a physical distance or clock comparison.
- Temporary simplifications are announced and later released: flat before curved, zero energy before nonzero energy, matter before radiation and vacuum.
- Audience questions are structural hinges, especially for light propagation, fixed enclosed mass, redshift energy, and the time coordinate.
- Concrete analogies carry the abstraction: football field, rubber sheet, projectile, guitar string, and piston.
- Newtonian reasoning is used as far as it reaches; the energy-curvature correspondence is explicitly reserved for Einstein's equations.

## Pacing Risks

- Do not compress the repeated metric and Hubble derivations; the repetition prepares the dynamical calculation.
- Do not make the three-sphere discussion imply an external center or an explosion into empty space.
- Do not erase the lecturer's algebra correction; retain the check while presenting only correct equations.
- Do not flatten matter, radiation, and vacuum energy into a table without their physical dilution arguments.
- Do not state the lecture's expansion-energy analogy as a universal global conservation theorem in general relativity.
- Keep the closing audience sequence in the chapter. It supplies the interpretation of redshift, pressure, coordinates, curvature, and cosmic fate.

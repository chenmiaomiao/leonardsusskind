# Narrative Map

## Opening Setup

The lecture begins with a deliberately possible but implausible collision law. Its failure under rotation supplies the criterion used throughout the chapter: write laws for whole objects that transform coherently, not for isolated coordinate components.

## Beat Sequence

1. **A bad law isolates isotropy.** Equality of two x-coordinates survives translations but fails when the axes rotate.
2. **Spatial coincidence repairs the law.** Three component equalities become one vector equation, and a zero vector stays zero in every rotated frame.
3. **Relativity enlarges coincidence to an event.** Equal times or equal spatial positions alone are frame-dependent; the entire spacetime separation must vanish.
4. **Boosts and rotations become matrices.** Explicit transformations establish the spatial-first component order, matrix composition, and Einstein summation.
5. **Scalars and the metric produce invariants.** Index lowering packages the Minkowski signs, while sums and differences prove the invariant inner product.
6. **Proper time repairs velocity.** Dividing spacetime displacement by proper time yields a unit timelike four-vector and then four-momentum.
7. **The Lorentz force exposes a six-component object.** A rest-frame argument shows that electric and magnetic fields must mix under boosts.
8. **Tensor rank is defined by transformation.** Products motivate component counts, but one Lorentz matrix per index supplies the actual definition.
9. **Antisymmetry gives exactly six slots.** Four diagonal zeros and sign-related off-diagonal entries match the combined electric and magnetic components.
10. **The field tensor completes the construction.** Electric components occupy space-time slots; magnetic components occupy the spatial block and follow a cyclic pattern.
11. **Historical questions return to the principle.** The room discusses Einstein's component treatment and Minkowski's geometric organization, with uncertainty retained where the lecturer states it.

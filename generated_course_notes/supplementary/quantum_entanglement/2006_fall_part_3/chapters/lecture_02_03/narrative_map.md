# Narrative Map

## Opening Logic

The recording begins from a reciprocal pair: fields act on charges through the Lorentz force, while charges and currents must act back on fields. Energy conservation makes the second half necessary, and causality turns a changing source into an outward-moving field disturbance. This physical argument must precede the formal equations.

## Lecture Sequence

1. Recall the three-vector and covariant Lorentz force laws, then identify Maxwell's equations as the missing source-to-field laws.
2. Use a displaced charge, an oscillating antenna, and a reversed wire current to explain why field rearrangements propagate as waves.
3. Review scalar wave equations, the \(c=1\) convention, directional profiles \(f(z-t)\) and \(g(z+t)\), and sinusoidal waves with wave number \(k\).
4. Rebuild dot products, cross products, \(\boldsymbol\nabla\), curl, and divergence before applying them to electromagnetism.
5. Write the four vacuum Maxwell equations, note their electric-magnetic near symmetry, and postpone \(\rho\) and \(\mathbf J\).
6. Assemble \(\mathbf E\) and \(\mathbf B\) into \(F^{\mu\nu}\), construct the dual tensor with the corrected sign rule, and count the equations in each free-index relation.
7. Verify spatial and time components explicitly so the compact tensor equations earn their claim to covariance.
8. Eliminate \(\mathbf B\) from the two first-order curl equations, perform the double-curl calculation, and recover a second-order wave equation for \(E_x\).
9. Build an \(x\)-polarized plane wave moving along \(z\), use divergence to eliminate a longitudinal wave component, and use curl to place \(\mathbf B\) along \(y\).
10. Restore charge density as a coarse-grained field and strengthen global conservation into local transport rather than instantaneous disappearance and reappearance.
11. Define current density through an oriented area, apply the fixed-region flux balance and Gauss's theorem, and derive the continuity equation.
12. Package \(\rho\) and \(\mathbf J\) into \(J^\mu\), restore the sourced Maxwell equations, and verify \(\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu}=J^\nu\).

## Characteristic Rhythm

- Physical necessity comes before formal notation: energy exchange, causal rearrangement, and flowing charge motivate the equations.
- Review material is functional rather than decorative. The scalar wave and vector-calculus passages supply every operation used later.
- Compact tensor equations are always unpacked into components before covariance is accepted.
- The difficult double-curl algebra is performed once rather than replaced by a slogan; only then is the wave equation interpreted.
- Sources are deliberately removed, the vacuum structure is understood, and sources are restored at the end.
- Board corrections remain part of the reasoning, especially the sign in the dual tensor and the distinction between Gauss's law and Gauss's theorem.

## Audience Exchanges

No source-confirmed audience exchange occurs. The lecturer asks several rhetorical questions and once asks whether anyone knows the divergence-theorem integrand, but no substantive audience response follows. These moments remain part of the exposition rather than being recast as Question and Answer blocks.

## Compression Risks

- Do not reduce the moved-charge and reversed-current arguments to the generic statement that accelerating charges radiate.
- Do not omit the general profiles \(f(z-t)\) and \(g(z+t)\) in favor of the cosine example alone.
- Preserve enough component detail in the curl, divergence, tensor, and double-curl calculations that the reader can reproduce the result.
- Keep the distinction between a constant longitudinal electric field and the absent longitudinal wave component.
- Preserve the Earth-to-Mars counterexample, the oriented surface element, the sign of outward flux, and the water-pipe picture behind the divergence theorem.
- End by joining both directions of electromagnetic coupling: fields act on charges, and conserved charge-current sources generate fields.

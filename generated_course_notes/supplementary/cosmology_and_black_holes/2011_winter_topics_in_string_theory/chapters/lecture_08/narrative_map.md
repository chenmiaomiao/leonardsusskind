# Narrative Map

## Opening Questions

The lecture begins in the quantum vacuum rather than in cosmology. The harmonic oscillator resolves the apparent conflict between an empty ground state and nonzero energy. Questions about the cosmological-constant problem and ultraviolet--infrared relations establish what will not be pursued.

## Symmetry Before Dynamics

The refractive-index question leads through translation, rotation, and Lorentz invariance. The laws can possess a symmetry that a material configuration breaks. Vacuum energy is special because its state preserves Lorentz invariance, so it selects no rest frame.

## Expansion in Three Cases

The lecture next contrasts matter, radiation, and vacuum density. Gravity makes the distinction consequential. The flat expanding metric and Friedmann equation reduce the problem to \(a(t)\). A comoving box gives the matter law and its \(t^{2/3}\) solution; a constant source gives the exponential solution.

## From Equations to the Future Sky

Matter and vacuum terms together explain a transition from deceleration to acceleration. The discussion then becomes operational: lookback time, a two-body vacuum detector, local binding, Andromeda, escape velocity, and the effective linear repulsion.

## Causal Geometry

After the break, the exponential metric is named de Sitter space. Flat and black-hole Penrose diagrams establish the definition of an event horizon. Conformal time places the infinite future at \(T=0\) and preserves \(45^\circ\) null rays.

## Alice, Bob, and the Horizon

Alice's limiting past light cone defines her private horizon. Bob can leave causal contact unless a binding interaction keeps their proper separation fixed. The last exchanges compare de Sitter and black-hole redshift, preview horizon temperature, return to the original time coordinate, and derive the fixed proper radius \(H^{-1}\).

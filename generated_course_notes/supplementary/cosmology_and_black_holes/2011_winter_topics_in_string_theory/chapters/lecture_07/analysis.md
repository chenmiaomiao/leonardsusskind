## Lecture Arc

The lecture first rebuilds the string-side count in natural units. Newton's constant is an area, string interactions contribute two powers of the amplitude-level coupling, and a long random-walk string has entropy \(S_{\mathrm{str}}\sim M\ell_s\).

The black-hole result \(S_{\mathrm{BH}}\sim M^2G_N\) cannot be obtained by simply calling a free string a black hole. Self-gravity changes both size and mass. The remedy is to vary \(g_s\) slowly and reversibly at fixed \(\ell_s\), following an isentrope \(Mg_s=\text{constant}\) until it meets the correspondence curve \(Mg_s^2\sim1/\ell_s\). Their intersection reproduces the area-law scaling.

The calculation motivates a stronger question: how much entropy can fit inside a region? Ordinary cell counting predicts a volume law. A shell-collapse argument and the second law instead give \(S_{\max}=A/(4G_N)\), the counting form of holography.

The final turn is cosmological. The flat expanding metric gives \(D=a\Delta x\), \(V=HD\), and a Hubble radius \(H^{-1}\). Constant \(H\) produces \(a(t)=a_0e^{Ht}\), introducing de Sitter space and its horizon.

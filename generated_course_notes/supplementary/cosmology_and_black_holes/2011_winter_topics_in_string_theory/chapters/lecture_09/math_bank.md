# Mathematics Bank

## Horizon Temperatures

\[
T_{\rm local}(\rho)\simeq\frac{1}{2\pi\rho},
\qquad
k_BT_{\rm local}\simeq\frac{\hbar c}{2\pi\rho}.
\]

\[
T_H=\frac{1}{8\pi M}
\quad(G_N=\hbar=c=k_B=1),
\qquad
T_H=\frac{\hbar c^3}{8\pi G_NMk_B}.
\]

For the de Sitter static patch,
\[
T_{\rm dS}=\frac{H}{2\pi},
\qquad
T_{\rm local}(r)=\frac{T_{\rm dS}}{\sqrt{1-H^2r^2}}.
\]

## de Sitter Geometry

\[
ds^2=-dt^2+e^{2Ht}d\mathbf{x}^2,
\qquad
D(t)=e^{Ht}\lvert\Delta\mathbf{x}\rvert.
\]

\[
\eta=-\frac{e^{-Ht}}{H},
\qquad
ds^2=\frac{-d\eta^2+d\mathbf{x}^2}{H^2\eta^2},
\qquad
\frac{dx}{d\eta}=\pm1.
\]

On the observer's horizon, \(\Delta x=\lvert\eta\rvert\), so
\[
D_{\rm hor}^2=\frac{(\Delta x)^2}{H^2\eta^2}
=\frac{1}{H^2},
\qquad
D_{\rm hor}=H^{-1}.
\]

The observer-adapted metric is
\[
ds^2=-(1-H^2r^2)d\tau^2
+\frac{dr^2}{1-H^2r^2}
+r^2d\Omega_2^2,
\]
with horizon \(r_H=H^{-1}\). Its redshift factor is structurally analogous to \(1-2G_NM/r\) in Schwarzschild coordinates.

## Vacuum Repulsion

The lecture begins with the schematic Poisson argument and the notes state its normalized completion:
\[
\nabla^2\phi=4\pi G_N\rho,
\qquad
\phi_{\rm dS}(r)=-\frac12H^2r^2,
\qquad
a_r=H^2r.
\]

For a local mass embedded in de Sitter space,
\[
\phi(r)=-\frac{G_NM}{r}-\frac12H^2r^2,
\qquad
a_r=-\frac{G_NM}{r^2}+H^2r.
\]
The unstable balance occurs at
\[
r_\ast=\left(\frac{G_NM}{H^2}\right)^{1/3}.
\]
The relativistic qualification is that homogeneous-fluid acceleration depends on \(\rho+3p\); vacuum energy has \(p=-\rho\).

## Scale and Thermodynamics

\[
H^2=\frac{8\pi G_N}{3}\rho_\Lambda,
\qquad
\rho_\Lambda\sim10^{-123}\rho_{\rm Pl},
\qquad
r_H=H^{-1}.
\]

\[
A_H=4\pi H^{-2},
\qquad
S_{\rm dS}=\frac{A_H}{4G_N}
=\frac{\pi}{G_NH^2}.
\]

For an entropy deficit \(\Delta S\),
\[
P\sim e^{-\Delta S},
\qquad
t\sim t_0e^{\Delta S},
\qquad
t_{\rm rec}\sim H^{-1}e^{S_{\rm dS}}.
\]

## Exponential Populations

In a doubling population, the fraction contained in the final \(n\) generations is
\[
f_n=1-2^{-n}.
\]
Thus the latest one, two, and three generations contain \(1/2\), \(3/4\), and \(7/8\) of the population. This elementary fact is the mathematical core of the lecture's cutoff-dominance examples.

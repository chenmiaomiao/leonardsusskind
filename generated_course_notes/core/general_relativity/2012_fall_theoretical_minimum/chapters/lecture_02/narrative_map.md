# Narrative Map

## Opening: Geometry Without an Outside View

The rolled page separates extrinsic bending from intrinsic distance. The surface observer and the triangulated lattice turn that distinction into measurable data. A forced bulge shows that incompatible link lengths encode real curvature.

## Coordinate Language

Two smooth inverse coordinate maps describe the same points. An oblique coordinate grid then gives basis vectors, contravariant expansion coefficients, covariant projections, and the metric as their Gram matrix. Polar coordinates prevent orthogonality from being mistaken for Cartesian normalization.

## Tensor Definition

The scalar, coordinate differential, and scalar gradient establish scalar, contravariant, and covariant transformation laws. Mixed and rank-two examples reveal the general rule: each free index carries one Jacobian of the orientation dictated by its position.

## Coordinate-Independent Algebra

Tensor equations preserve truth because transformation laws are linear. Addition requires matching tensor type. Tensor products retain ordered free slots. Contraction becomes legal only for one upper and one lower index, since inverse Jacobians then collapse to the mixed identity.

## Return to Geometry

The line element defines the metric abstractly. Symmetry yields d(d+1)/2 independent components. Scalar invariance of distance proves the covariant metric transformation law.

## Closing: Inverse Metric and Curvature Ahead

The metric is a nondegenerate symmetric matrix with inverse g superscript mn. Tangent-space language and a two-dimensional matrix expansion clarify the identity relation. The Riemannian and Lorentzian caveats then lead directly to covariant differentiation and curvature.

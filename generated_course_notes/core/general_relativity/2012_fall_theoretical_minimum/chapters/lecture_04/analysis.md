# Lecture Analysis

## Central Problem

The lecture derives the local metric and Newtonian equation of motion seen in a uniformly accelerated elevator. Its central result is that flat Minkowski spacetime in Rindler coordinates reproduces a uniform gravitational force while retaining zero curvature.

## Expository Structure

1. Covariant differentiation is reviewed for lower and upper vector indices.
2. The covariant differential becomes the rule for parallel transport.
3. Parallel transport of the tangent defines a geodesic.
4. Euclidean distance is replaced by Lorentzian proper time.
5. Equal coordinate acceleration is rejected because it is not relativistically rigid.
6. Euclidean polar coordinates motivate hyperbolic Rindler coordinates.
7. Fixed-radius hyperbolae have constant proper acceleration inversely proportional to radius.
8. The Rindler metric is expanded near a reference observer with terrestrial acceleration.
9. The slow-motion spacetime geodesic equation reduces to Newtonian free fall.
10. The weak-field time component previews the Schwarzschild radius.

## Editorial Decisions

The chapter follows all substantive movements in chronological order and retains every identifiable classroom question or correction. Repeated oral restatements are consolidated only when they introduce no new argument, qualification, or example.

The lecture's mostly-plus tensor convention is stated before signs are normalized. The exact Rindler metric is kept distinct from its local weak-field expansion. The Schwarzschild discussion is labeled a heuristic preview because the weak-field rule cannot derive the exact strong-field solution or its radial metric component.

# Narrative Map

## Opening: Why the Machinery Is Visible

General relativity is presented as conceptually manageable but notationally and computationally demanding. Compact formalisms and symbolic packages are acknowledged, while component indices remain the working language.

## The Flatness Question

The transition from pure Riemannian geometry to gravity turns on one question: is an apparent field genuine or only a coordinate effect? Searching every coordinate system is rejected in favor of a tensor built from the metric and its derivatives.

## Local Flatness

The metric review leads to normal coordinates. At one point the metric can be made Euclidean and its first derivatives zero. The quadratic coordinate expansion and component count explain why first-order variation can be removed and why second-order information is the natural home of curvature.

## Differentiation Across Changing Bases

A constant geometric vector acquires changing components in curvilinear coordinates. Ordinary component derivatives therefore fail to transform tensorially. Differentiating first in a normal frame and then transforming back yields the covariant derivative and the Christoffel correction.

## Connection from the Metric

The rank-two derivative rule is applied to the metric. Metric compatibility, together with torsion-free symmetry, produces three permuted equations whose combination isolates the Levi-Civita connection.

## Curvature and Gravity

The cut-and-glued cone makes holonomy visible. Two infinitesimal transport orders become a commutator of covariant derivatives, defining the Riemann tensor. A linked object entering a curved region then turns holonomy into tidal stress, completing the bridge from geometry to gravity.

# Lecture 5 Source Analysis

## Central Argument

The chapter follows one continuous problem.

1. Tensor equations preserve equality and vanishing under coordinate changes.
2. A scalar gradient is tensorial, but an ordinary vector-component derivative is not.
3. The product rule isolates the extra term caused by a moving coordinate basis.
4. A connection cancels that term and produces the covariant derivative.
5. Metric compatibility and zero torsion determine the Levi--Civita connection.
6. Contracting the covariant derivative with a tangent defines differentiation along a curve.
7. A geodesic is a curve whose tangent is covariantly constant.
8. In spacetime, the geodesic equation provides the first bridge from metric derivatives to gravity.

## Source Corrections

- The early board coefficient for a lower-index vector enters with a plus sign. It is denoted by \(C\) in the chapter and related to the conventional Christoffel symbol by \(C=-\Gamma\). All later formulas use the standard Levi--Civita convention consistently.
- Lower indices therefore contribute minus connection terms, while upper indices contribute plus terms.
- Metric compatibility is expanded with the standard signs.
- The Christoffel formula is stated for the torsion-free, metric-compatible connection.
- Local flatness is exact at a chosen point through first derivatives, not throughout a finite curved region.
- Vanishing connection coefficients at one point do not imply flatness.
- In polar coordinates, \(g_{\theta\theta}=r^2\) varies; the transcript's reference to \(g_{rr}=r^2\) is corrected.
- The exterior derivative of a one-form corresponds to curl in three dimensions only after the usual Euclidean identifications and Hodge dual.
- The geodesic parameter is identified as affine; arc length or proper time supplies the lecture's principal example.

# Lecture 5 Narrative Map

## 1. Coordinate-Independent Truth

Begin with compatible tensor algebra, then recover the reason tensors are useful: components support calculation while tensor equations preserve geometric truth.

## 2. Scalar Control and Vector Failure

Use the scalar chain rule as the successful control case. Contrast it with a constant vector in a moving curvilinear frame, then preserve the full product-rule calculation that disproves tensoriality of the ordinary derivative.

## 3. Connection and Index Rules

Turn the extra term into a correction coefficient. Explain the temporary board sign before adopting standard Levi--Civita notation. Generalize one index at a time and retain the classroom corrections distinguishing free from dummy indices.

## 4. Polar Geometry

Use the radial vector field to show that constant components need not mean a constant vector. Compute the relevant polar Christoffel symbols so the geometric correction is explicit.

## 5. Metric Compatibility

Motivate \(\nabla g=0\) from homogeneous Cartesian space, add the torsion-free condition, and derive the standard Christoffel formula. Preserve questions about notation, symmetry, coordinate choice, and the non-tensorial character of the connection.

## 6. Local Flatness

Interpret covariant differentiation through normal coordinates at one point. Distinguish vanishing connection coefficients at a point from flatness over a region, and separate the exterior derivative from the connection-based derivative.

## 7. Curves and Geodesics

Introduce arc length, the unit tangent, scalar differentiation along a path, and then vector differentiation along a path. Insert the tangent itself to obtain the geodesic equation.

## 8. Gravity Ahead

End where the lecture ends: compare geodesic coordinate acceleration with Newtonian gravitational acceleration, then point forward to parallel transport and curvature.

# Lecture 7 Source Analysis

## Central Argument

The lecture follows one continuous transition from geometry to matter.

1. Infinitesimal holonomy turns two-dimensional curvature into a local experiment.
2. In higher dimensions, the loop plane and the vector-space rotation must both be recorded.
3. The resulting multilinear map is the Riemann tensor.
4. Its symmetries, Bianchi identity, and Ricci contraction organize the independent curvature data.
5. Charge density and current introduce local conservation in its simplest relativistic form.
6. Energy and momentum require a rank-two density-and-flux tensor.
7. Stress--energy supplies the matter side of gravity, while the geodesic equation supplies the motion side.
8. The weak-field limit identifies the connection component that reproduces Newtonian acceleration.

## Source Corrections

- The two directed loop edges are represented by an area bivector rather than a bare product of coordinate increments.
- A repeated-index collision in the live board discussion is normalized to four distinct Riemann slots.
- One explicit sign convention is fixed for the commutator and Riemann tensor.
- Normal coordinates remove first metric derivatives at one point; second derivatives generally remain and encode curvature.
- The Levi--Civita Riemann symmetries include the first Bianchi identity.
- The independent component counts are corrected to \(1\), \(6\), and \(20\) in dimensions \(2\), \(3\), and \(4\).
- The small path is required to close; higher-order coordinate-rectangle corrections do not alter the leading holonomy term.
- Historical speculation about Ricci is replaced by the precise three-dimensional reconstruction formula.
- The capacitor exchange uses \(Q=CV\) and \(U=\tfrac12CV^2\), distinguishing separated plate charge from net charge.
- Tensoriality of stress--energy is not inferred merely from the physical names of its components.
- Flat-space conservation is distinguished from \(\nabla_\mu T^{\nu\mu}=0\) in curved space-time.
- The slow-motion spatial geodesic equation retains the required minus sign, \(d^2x^i/dt^2\simeq-\Gamma^i{}_{00}\).

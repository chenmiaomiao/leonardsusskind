# Narrative Map

## Sequence

1. Position-dependent acceleration makes a stationary Rindler congruence possible.
2. Hyperbolic coordinates produce the exact Rindler metric and proper-time relation.
3. Alice crosses the acceleration horizon in finite proper time while Bob sees asymptotic approach.
4. The Schwarzschild exterior has a coordinate singularity at \(r=2GM\) and true curvature divergence at \(r=0\).
5. A deliberately singular flat-space coordinate demonstrates why divergent components are not sufficient evidence of curvature.
6. Proper radial distance and rescaled time reduce the near-horizon metric to Rindler form.
7. Exterior signals become delayed, redshifted, and energetically faint while infall remains locally regular.
8. Nearby freely falling observers communicate normally through the horizon; no timelike observer can remain on it.
9. Inside, fixed \(r\) is spacelike and the singularity is a future boundary reached in finite proper time.
10. Tidal curvature distinguishes stellar and supermassive horizon crossings.
11. Vacuum Einstein equations determine the spherical exterior just as the source-free Poisson equation determines \(-GM/r\).
12. Newtonian angular momentum can prevent point collision but cannot provide an outward future after horizon crossing.
13. Liouville incompressibility, quantum pressure, and horizon-scale mean density are distinct ideas.
14. A fixed-density radius grows as \(M^{1/3}\), while the Schwarzschild radius grows as \(M\).
15. A de Sitter cosmic horizon is an inside-out causal analogue, not a Schwarzschild interior.
16. Approaching the singularity removes causal communication on successively smaller physical scales.
17. Meter sticks and reflected signals provide operational checks that nothing local happens at the horizon.
18. The exterior slicing can represent infalling matter as thin layers near the horizon without requiring signals from inside.
19. Kerr rotation changes the exterior geometry but leaves mass and angular momentum bounded global data.

## Voice and Rhythm

The chapter follows the lecture's alternation between diagrams, derivations, and audience challenges. Live notation drift is normalized without rearranging the explanatory sequence. The result reads as first-prepared lecture notes rather than commentary about how the notes were produced.

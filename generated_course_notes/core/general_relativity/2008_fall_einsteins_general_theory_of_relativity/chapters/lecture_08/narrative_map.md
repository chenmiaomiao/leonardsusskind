# Lecture 8 Narrative Map

## 00:00--00:20: Operator Preparation

Curvature is reintroduced as failed return under parallel transport. The
lecture temporarily adopts operator language: derivatives, multiplication
functions, and matrices all act linearly on vector fields. Treating each
\(\Gamma_\mu\) as a matrix yields
\(\nabla_\mu=\partial_\mu+\Gamma_\mu\). The commutator lemma
\([\partial_x,F]=F'\) is then proved by the product rule.

## 00:20--00:49: Holonomy and Riemann

Two infinitesimal edge vectors define a loop. Values of a parallel-transported
vector at the four corners are arranged into a difference of differences.
The cancellation leaves the initial--final mismatch, and the infinitesimal
limit becomes \([\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu]V\). Expanding the operators produces
the derivative and quadratic connection terms of the Riemann tensor.

## 00:49--01:11: Symmetry, Contraction, and Flatness

Questions about the selected plane and the shrinking loop clarify that
curvature is local change per oriented area. Lowering an index exposes the
Riemann pair symmetries and Bianchi identity. Same-pair contractions vanish;
the mixed contraction gives Ricci, and a second contraction gives the scalar.
Dimension-dependent flatness criteria lead into intrinsic versus extrinsic
curvature.

## 01:11--01:32: Geodesics Meet Newton

The lecture pivots to slowly moving particles in weak, nearly static fields.
Proper time becomes coordinate time to leading order, so the spatial geodesic
equation keeps only \(\Gamma^i{}_{00}\). Expanding that connection with
signature \(+---\) gives
\(\mathbf a=-\tfrac12\boldsymbol{\nabla}g_{00}\), hence
\(g_{00}=1+2\Phi\).

## 01:32--01:46: From Poisson to Einstein

Newtonian Gauss and Poisson equations connect \(\Phi\) to mass density, which
becomes \(T_{00}\) relativistically. The resulting component equation is
correct but frame-dependent. Requiring a two-index curvature tensor suggests
\(A R_{\mu\nu}+B g_{\mu\nu}R\), and the lecture closes by previewing the
Einstein-tensor coefficients.

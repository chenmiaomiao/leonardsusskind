# Lecture 8 Editorial Analysis

## Lecture Arc

The lecture has two linked halves. The first turns infinitesimal holonomy
into the Riemann tensor by treating the covariant derivative as an operator.
The second takes the geodesic equation into the weak-field, slow-motion
regime and reconstructs the need for a covariant gravitational field
equation from Newtonian gravity.

The accepted chapter follows the original order:

1. derivative, function-multiplication, and matrix operators;
2. the shorthand \(\nabla_\mu=\partial_\mu+\Gamma_\mu\);
3. the commutator identity \([\partial_x,F]=F'\);
4. loop transport as a difference of differences;
5. the Riemann tensor from \([\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu]\);
6. Riemann symmetries, Ricci contraction, and scalar curvature;
7. intrinsic versus extrinsic curvature;
8. slow geodesics and the weak-field metric;
9. \(g_{00}=1+2\Phi\) and Poisson's equation;
10. the tensor ansatz leading to the Einstein tensor.

## Source Corrections

- One explicit convention fixes the sign of the Riemann tensor and loop
  holonomy. Reversing either the commutator order or loop orientation is
  stated to reverse the result.
- The oriented loop area is represented by a bivector rather than an
  unqualified product of edge lengths.
- The first Bianchi identity answers the classroom question about a
  curvature sum rule.
- The exact independent-component counts are \(1,6,20\) in dimensions
  \(2,3,4\), replacing the rough four-dimensional count voiced live.
- Scalar-flat, Ricci-flat, and Riemann-flat conditions are distinguished by
  dimension. The saddle and great-circle exchanges are corrected without
  losing their pedagogical role.
- The \(+---\) signature is carried through the weak-field calculation,
  giving \(d^2x^i/dt^2=-\tfrac12\partial_i g_{00}\) and
  \(g_{00}=1+2\Phi\).
- \(T_{00}\simeq T^{00}\simeq\rho\) is explicitly restricted to the
  weak-field, slow-motion frame with \(c=1\).
- The final tensor equation remains a target rather than a completed
  derivation; the coefficients \(1\) and \(-\tfrac12\) are identified as a
  preview for the following lecture.

## Fidelity Decisions

All substantive derivations and classroom exchanges are retained. Repeated
phrases, pauses, false starts, and recognition noise are not reproduced.
Where the board calculation changes sign or index order while being worked
out, the notes preserve the question being resolved and then state one
consistent final convention.

Twenty-two frames were selected after a complete fifteen-second survey of
the video. They cover every major board phase. Institutional title cards,
frames dominated by the lecturer, incomplete early equation states,
duplicate views, and active erasures were rejected.

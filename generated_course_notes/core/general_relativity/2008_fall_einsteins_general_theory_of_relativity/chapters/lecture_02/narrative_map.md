# Narrative Map

## 00:00:14--00:14:15: Dark-Energy Detour

A weak repulsive term is compared with a spring, then separated from a
time-growing Big Rip scenario. Questions distinguish local density from
cosmic abundance, constants from mass-dependent quantities, and dark energy
from particle dark matter.

## 00:14:15--00:32:08: Newtonian Fields

Del, gradients, and divergences supply the notation. A test mass defines the
gravitational acceleration field, the source-directed displacement fixes its
sign, and a continuous mass density leads to Gauss's law.

## 00:32:08--00:42:31: Gauss's Theorem and Spherical Symmetry

The mathematical divergence theorem converts volume divergence to boundary
flux. Combined with the physical source law and isotropy, it recovers the
exterior inverse-square field.

## 00:42:31--00:58:54: Gravity Inside the Earth

A uniform-density model gives a linear interior field and harmonic motion
through an ideal tunnel. Questions develop the shell theorem, surface
discontinuities, radius scaling, and the live normalization dispute.

## 00:58:54--01:17:17: Accelerated Coordinates

The horizontal elevator makes uniform acceleration look like gravity.
Galilean coordinates are developed first for constant velocity and then for
the parabolic worldlines of a uniformly accelerated frame.

## 01:17:17--01:28:24: Light Bending

The equivalence principle is applied to light. A longitudinal beam motivates
frequency shift, while a sun-skimming ray gives the Newtonian deflection
scale and exposes the missing factor of two supplied by general relativity.

## 01:28:24--01:36:39: Local Equivalence and Tides

No single acceleration removes the Earth's radial field globally. Relative
accelerations stretch and squeeze an extended body, making tidal forces the
obstruction to global equivalence.

## 01:36:39--01:47:20: Metrics and Curvature

Neighboring distances define geometry. Curvilinear coordinates introduce
the metric; planes, spheres, cylinders, saddles, and cones distinguish
coordinate complexity from intrinsic curvature. Curvature and tidal forces
emerge as parallel obstructions.

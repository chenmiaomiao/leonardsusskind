# Lecture 3 Narrative Map

## 1. Vertical Light

Return to the elevator, replace the bullet by light, and move the physical
question from speed to frequency. Derive the weak shift from the velocity
acquired during one light-travel time.

## 2. Free Fall

Use the equivalence principle twice: first to identify the accelerated
elevator shift with gravitational redshift, then to show why a falling
laboratory cancels that shift locally. Preserve the classroom challenge
about exact cancellation.

## 3. Locality and Tides

Interpret apparent uniform gravity as an accelerated coordinate choice.
Then show why the varying field around a source defeats one global
accelerated frame. This is the physical entrance to curvature.

## 4. Coordinate Calculus

Introduce a scalar field, infinitesimal displacement, total differential,
summation convention, smooth coordinate maps, the chain rule, and inverse
Jacobians. Keep the live questions about finite differences, smoothness, and
dimension.

## 5. Tensor Laws

Separate geometric objects from coordinate components. Move in the same
order as the board: scalar, displacement vector, general contravariant
vector, rank-two contravariant tensor, gradient covector, contraction, and
rank-two covariant tensor.

## 6. The Metric

Restart from Cartesian Pythagoras after the break. Introduce the Kronecker
delta, transform the line element, and identify the metric as a covariant
rank-two tensor.

## 7. Flatness and Examples

Define flatness on a region, distinguish pointwise from finite-neighborhood
flattening, and use the cone as a local-global warning. Derive the polar
metric in the flat plane, answer the off-diagonal question, and close with
the metric of the sphere.

# Editorial Analysis

## Lecture Arc

Lecture 10 begins with a low-dimensional geometry that can be seen before it is calculated: a point mass in two spatial dimensions produces a conical defect. The dimensional discussion then tests why familiar gravitational, orbital, atomic, and nuclear structure favors three spatial dimensions, while an audience question restores time to the spacetime picture through Lorentz transformations.

The central technical movement reconstructs the Einstein tensor from curvature and covariant conservation, then identifies the cosmological-constant ambiguity through metric compatibility. The equation is presented as a constrained physical postulate rather than a theorem, with its Newtonian limit and later empirical success kept explicit.

The equivalence principle becomes a working recipe. A flat-space scalar wave is rewritten in tensor form and promoted to curved spacetime. The direction of influence is then reversed: the wave carries stress-energy and must source gravity. Conservation fixes the relative coefficient in the scalar stress tensor, and its 00 component recovers positive kinetic and gradient energy.

The closing third remains question-driven. Pressure and shear are interpreted as gravitational sources; localized matter is separated from its nonlocal exterior field; weak light self-gravity is distinguished from exact superposition; and the cosmological constant is treated as long-range acceleration, vacuum stress, and the source of Einstein's unstable static-universe construction. The lecture closes by previewing Schwarzschild geometry, correcting the photon-sphere radius, and separating spatial flatness from flat spacetime.

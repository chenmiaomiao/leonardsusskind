# Mathematical Record

## Transcript and Board Results

- Newton's law: \(\vec F=m\vec a\).
- Component kinematics: \(F_i=m\ddot x_i\).
- Uniform gravity: \(F_2=-mg\) and \(\ddot x_2=-g\).
- Two-body magnitude: \(F=GmM/r^2\).
- Circular acceleration: \(a=\omega^2r\), giving \(T^2\propto r^3\).
- Vector gravity: \(\vec F=-GmM\vec r/r^3\).
- Many-body gravity: sum the pair forces as vectors.
- Test-particle field: \(\vec g=\vec F_{\rm test}/m_{\rm test}\).
- Divergence:
  \(\nabla\cdot\vec A=\partial_xA_x+\partial_yA_y+\partial_zA_z\).
- Gauss's theorem:
  \(\int_V\nabla\cdot\vec A\,d^3x
    =\oint_{\partial V}\vec A\cdot\hat n\,d\sigma\).
- Spherical flux: \(4\pi r^2A(r)=Q\).
- Exterior spherical field: \(\vec g=-GM\hat r/r^2\).

## Standard Completions

- Relative free-fall acceleration vanishes in a uniform field.
- The tidal tensor is the gradient \(\partial_jg_i\); for a point source its
  radial eigenvalue is \(2GM/r^3\) and its two transverse eigenvalues are
  \(-GM/r^3\).
- Newtonian mass density obeys
  \(\nabla\cdot\vec g=-4\pi G\rho\).
- Inside a uniform sphere,
  \(\vec g=-(4\pi G\rho/3)\vec r\).

## Source Corrections

- The board's many-body sign ambiguity is removed by defining
  \(\vec r_{ij}=\vec r_j-\vec r_i\).
- The spoken surface-area slip is corrected to \(4\pi r^2\).
- Gauss's theorem alone gives zero flux inside a shell; zero field follows
  only after spherical symmetry is used as well.
- The flow model is explicitly identified as a mathematical analogy, not a
  physical claim that space is a fluid.

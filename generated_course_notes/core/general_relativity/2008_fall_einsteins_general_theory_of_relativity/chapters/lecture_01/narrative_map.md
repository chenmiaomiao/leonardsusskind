# Narrative Map

## 00:00:14--00:15:00: Newton, Galileo, and Universal Acceleration

Gravity is framed as the route to geometry, but the lecture begins with
inertial frames, vector kinematics, Newton's law, the flat-Earth
approximation, and the cancellation of the falling body's mass.

## 00:15:01--00:22:44: Free Fall as an Internal Experiment

The falling cloud converts mass cancellation into preserved relative motion.
Questions about lasers, the floor, and the sensation of acceleration clarify
what a self-contained freely falling laboratory can measure.

## 00:22:45--00:43:27: The Full Newtonian Force Law

The inverse-square magnitude, the weakness of gravity, equal and opposite
forces, Kepler's clue, contact-force limitations, vector direction, the
many-body sum, and the live displacement-sign correction.

## 00:43:28--01:01:17: Nonuniform Gravity, Tides, and Fields

Changing many-body accelerations lead to radial stretching, transverse
compression, lunar tides, and finally the gravitational field as test-particle
acceleration at each point.

## 01:01:18--01:22:33: Divergence and Gauss's Theorem

The lecture introduces divergence as a scalar derivative, builds its
intuition through incompressible water flow, handles the compressibility
caveat, and relates interior source strength to boundary flux.

## 01:22:34--01:31:20: Spherical Symmetry

A localized spherical source and a Gaussian sphere turn flux conservation
into a radial inverse-square field. Gravity reverses the sign and behaves
mathematically like flow into a sink.

## 01:31:21--01:38:27: Newton's Theorem and Its Domain

The exterior field depends only on total spherical mass. Mine-shaft behavior
shows the limitation at interior points, while a hollow shell gives exactly
zero interior field and anticipates the spherical exterior theorem of
general relativity.

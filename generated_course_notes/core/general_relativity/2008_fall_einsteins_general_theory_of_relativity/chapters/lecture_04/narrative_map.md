# Lecture 4 Narrative Map

## 1. Technical Minimum

Open with the reason for doing the algebra rather than merely stating its
conclusions. Define covariant and contravariant components by the two
chain-rule archetypes and preserve the question about whether they are
initially the same object.

## 2. Tensor Rank and Contraction

Move from one index to covariant and mixed rank-two laws. Derive trace
invariance, generalize contraction, and retain the questions about
factorization and geometric meaning.

## 3. Metric and Inverse

Introduce distance as a scalar quadratic form. Derive the metric
transformation law, translate the metric into matrix language, introduce
the inverse, and work the polar-coordinate example.

## 4. Index Motion

Use the metric to map vectors to covectors and raise an index on a
higher-rank tensor. Follow the live displacement contraction through its
explicit correction and finish with the three interval formulas.

## 5. Tensor Equations

State the matching-index rule for addition, prove that zero-ness and tensor
equality are coordinate independent, and stop before tensor differentiation.

## 6. Proper Time

Restart with Cartesian frames and interval-preserving transformations. Move
to the Lorentzian proper-time interval, explain \(c=1\), and retain the
natural-units question.

## 7. Spacetime Metrics

Introduce Greek indices, the general line element, Minkowski coordinates,
the eta metric, and Lorentzian signature. Preserve the questions about
coordinate labels and the inverse of eta.

## 8. Expansion

Write a consistent homogeneous scale-factor metric. Explain physical versus
comoving separation, then use the plane, sphere, horn, and arbitrary radial
profiles to make changing metric coefficients geometrically visible.

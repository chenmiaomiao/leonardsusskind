# Lecture 6 Source Analysis

## Central Argument

The chapter follows one continuous geometric argument.

1. Proper time extends metric geometry from space to space-time.
2. Accelerated coordinates can imitate gravity without creating curvature.
3. At every regular point, quadratic coordinate freedom removes the first metric derivatives and the connection.
4. This local simplification cannot generally be extended throughout a finite region.
5. Parallel transport compares directions along a chosen curve.
6. Closed-loop transport detects the obstruction to global local-inertial coordinates.
7. Cones, spheres, saddles, and tori separate local curvature, singular curvature, and topology.
8. In general relativity, this invariant curvature is the part of gravity that no accelerated frame removes.

## Source Corrections

- Natural units and restored-\(c\) formulas for proper time are kept dimensionally consistent.
- Constant metric components throughout a region are distinguished from normal-coordinate conditions at one point.
- Matching the number of quadratic coefficients to the number of first-derivative conditions is identified as a heuristic; the normal-coordinate construction supplies solvability.
- Metric compatibility is treated as a defining property of the Levi--Civita connection, not as an independent noncircular derivation.
- The normal-coordinate argument assumes a smooth, nondegenerate metric.
- Polar-coordinate failure at the origin is separated from the genuine singular geometry of a cone apex.
- The standard geodesic equation is stated for an affine parameter.
- Small contractible-loop holonomy diagnoses local curvature, while finite-loop effects may also reflect topology.
- The ideal cone has delta-function curvature with finite integrated angular deficit, rather than an ordinary infinite pointwise value.
- The board symbol \(R\) in \(\delta\theta=Ra\) is identified as a two-dimensional curvature coefficient and written as Gaussian curvature \(K\) in the notes.
- A flat quotient torus is distinguished from the curved doughnut embedded in three dimensions.
- Intrinsic parallel transport is distinguished from keeping an ambient-space vector fixed.

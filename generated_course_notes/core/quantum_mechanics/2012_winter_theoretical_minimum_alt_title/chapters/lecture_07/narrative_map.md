# Narrative Map

## Course Rhythm

1. **Correct the slogan.** Entanglement is not merely two systems sharing a state; it is failure of product factorization.
2. **Choose coordinates.** A wave function is the coefficient list of a state in a complete basis.
3. **Make predictions.** Expand expectation values into wave functions and observable matrix elements.
4. **Separate local actions.** Embed Alice and Bob observables with spectator identities.
5. **Test product structure.** Product states factor local and joint expectations; connected correlations expose nonfactorization.
6. **Use the singlet.** Local polarization vanishes while equal-axis outcomes are perfectly anticorrelated.
7. **Prepare the state.** A spin-spin Hamiltonian and energy relaxation connect the abstract singlet to laboratory physics.
8. **Trace out Bob.** Alice's reduced density matrix contains every Alice-local prediction.
9. **Read the spectrum.** Rank one means a product pure state; multiple Schmidt values mean entanglement.
10. **Model measurement.** A detector records the spin by becoming entangled with it.
11. **Move the cut.** Apparatus, eye, brain, cat, Schrodinger, and Wigner form one consistent hierarchy.
12. **Protect locality.** Bob changes conditional correlations but not Alice's unconditioned marginal statistics.
13. **State Bell's problem.** Shared local classical records cannot reproduce all entangled correlations after separation.
14. **Return to observables.** Ensembles and joint apparatuses distinguish local mixedness from global structure.

## Pacing Controls

- Do not skip the expectation-value algebra; it motivates the partial trace.
- Keep operator action separate from the physical act of measurement.
- Let the singlet carry both the mathematical and conceptual argument.
- Keep weak coupling separate from a failed detector-to-observer channel.
- Derive no signaling before discussing Bell nonlocality.
- End with the ensemble requirement so one event is never mistaken for tomography.

# Lecture 3 Source Analysis

## Central Problem

The chapter builds the mathematical bridge from state vectors to measurement. A basis turns an abstract operator into a matrix; Hermitian conjugation identifies the operator analogue of complex conjugation; Hermitian spectra then supply real measurement outcomes and mutually distinguishable eigenstates.

## Narrative Arc

1. Use an orthonormal basis to extract vector components and the completeness relation.
2. Define linear operators by homogeneity and additivity.
3. Derive matrix multiplication from the abstract equation \(M|A\rangle=|B\rangle\).
4. Define left action on bras and the Hermitian conjugate \(M^\dagger\).
5. Establish the structure and spectral properties of Hermitian operators.
6. State observables, eigenvalues, eigenstates, amplitudes, probabilities, normalization, and measurement preparation.
7. Reconstruct \(\sigma_z\), \(\sigma_x\), and \(\sigma_y\) from spin experiments.

## Source Cautions

- Complex conjugation is anti-linear; it is not an ordinary ket-to-ket linear operator.
- The two-dimensional rotation example concerns real eigendirections. Complexification changes that statement.
- The transcript briefly mistranscribes the Hermitian diagonal condition; the surrounding derivation and board show that diagonal entries are real.
- Degenerate eigenvalues do not by themselves force chosen eigenvectors to be orthogonal, although an orthonormal basis may be selected in each eigenspace.
- The in/out sign convention is stated hesitantly. The notes use the normalized convention consistent with the completed \(\sigma_y\) matrix.
- The arbitrary-direction spin probability is announced but deliberately left for the next lecture.

## Mathematical Spine

1. $[L,M]=0$ from a complete common eigenbasis, together with the converse simultaneous-diagonalization statement.
2. $U^\dagger U=I$, $U(\epsilon)=I-i\epsilon H$, and $H^\dagger=H$.
3. $d|\psi\rangle/dt=-iH|\psi\rangle$ and $H|E_i\rangle=E_i|E_i\rangle$.
4. $d\langle L\rangle/dt=-i\langle[L,H]\rangle$ and the commutator product rule.
5. $\alpha_j(t)=\alpha_j(0)e^{-iE_jt}$ and the transition-frequency matrix formula.
6. $H=\omega\sigma_z/2$, the Pauli commutators, and spin precession.
7. $H=(\omega/2)\bm\sigma\cdot\bm n$ with energies $\pm\omega/2$.

## Editorial Cautions

- The loose shorthand $\dot L=-i[L,H]$ remains subordinate to the exact expectation-value equation.
- The single-spin arrow is identified as an expectation-value picture, not a simultaneously definite classical vector.
- An additive identity in the Hamiltonian is described as a common phase, not literally as no mathematical effect.
- Photon emission requires coupling to the radiation field and is not part of the isolated precession Hamiltonian.
- Statistical averages are not presented as guaranteed outcomes of every finite experimental sequence.

# Chapter Plan

## Source Arc

- Reopen the previous lecture's photon-emission question and distinguish discrete quantum events from the continuous classical energy loss recovered by an ensemble average.
- State the collapse rule operationally, then expose the system-apparatus split that motivates treating both as one quantum system.
- Construct the tensor-product state space from paired orthonormal bases, dimensions, inner products, and arbitrary linear combinations.
- Specialize to two spins, distinguish product states from general states by coefficient factorization and parameter counting, and define entanglement.
- Embed one-spin observables in the composite space, establish spectator-index action, and prove that operators on distinct factors commute.
- Develop the singlet from vanishing local polarization, preparation by antiferromagnetic relaxation, total spin, and basis independence.
- Calculate the singlet correlation tensor and close with Bell's question: correlations defeat local classical simulation but do not enable signaling.

## Mathematical Spine

1. \(H=\omega\sigma_z/2\), \(E_\pm=\pm\omega/2\), and all-or-nothing photon emission with mean energy \(|\alpha|^2\omega\).
2. \(\ket{\psi}=\sum_i\alpha_i\ket{\lambda_i}\) and the operational collapse rule \(\ket{\psi}\to\ket{\lambda_k}\).
3. \(\mathcal S_{AI}=\mathcal S_A\otimes\mathcal S_I\), multiplied dimensions, factorized inner products, and a general coefficient array \(c_{ai}\).
4. Product-state factorization, \(c_{uu}c_{dd}=c_{ud}c_{du}\), and the six-versus-four real-parameter comparison.
5. Local embeddings \(\sigma_m\otimes I\) and \(I\otimes\sigma_n\), with \([\sigma_m,\tau_n]=0\).
6. \(\ket S=(\ket{ud}-\ket{du})/\sqrt2\), zero local Bloch vectors, and basis-independent antisymmetry.
7. \(\langle\sigma_m\tau_n\rangle_S=-\delta_{mn}\) and \(\langle(\bm\sigma\cdot\bm a)(\bm\tau\cdot\bm b)\rangle_S=-\bm a\cdot\bm b\).

## Editorial Cautions

- Photon emission is treated as a quantum event coupled to the radiation field, not as the ideal repeatable measurement of a spin component.
- The collapse rule is presented as operationally successful without claiming that the conceptual measurement problem has been solved.
- The tensor product is basis independent even though its coordinates are constructed from chosen factor bases.
- The claim that interaction produces entanglement is qualified: generic interactions can do so, but special states, times, and Hamiltonians need not.
- The singlet's \(yy\) correlation is \(-1\); a spoken zero is corrected using the explicit Pauli action and rotationally invariant result.
- Bell correlations are distinguished from both ordinary shared-classical correlations and usable faster-than-light signaling.

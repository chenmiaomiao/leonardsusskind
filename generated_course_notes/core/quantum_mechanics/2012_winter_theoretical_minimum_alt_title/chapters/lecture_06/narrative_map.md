# Narrative Map

## 00:00:09 to 00:10:30

The lecture returns to a sideways spin in a magnetic field. A classical rotor radiates continuously and loses an angle-dependent amount of energy; a quantum spin emits either one full photon or none. The probability-weighted mean reproduces the classical answer. Questions add lifetime broadening and explain why spontaneous emission is not an ideal repeatable spin measurement.

## 00:10:30 to 00:16:40

The collapse rule is written in an observable's eigenbasis. Its predictive success is separated from the conceptual division between system and apparatus. Treating the apparatus quantum mechanically points toward one larger state space governed by unitary dynamics.

## 00:16:40 to 00:25:00

Two abstract systems are assigned Hilbert spaces and orthonormal bases. Pairing every basis state produces the tensor-product basis, dimensions multiply, and inner products factor. Questions distinguish basis vectors from arbitrary vectors and coordinates from the basis-independent composite space.

## 00:25:00 to 00:41:50

The construction is applied to two spins labeled by \(\sigma\) and \(\tau\). Separately prepared spins give product states; expanding them in the four-state basis reveals a rank-one coefficient constraint. Parameter counting shows why most pure two-spin states cannot factor and are therefore entangled.

## 00:41:50 to 01:03:00

One-spin observables are embedded with an identity on the spectator factor. Their action on composite basis states is calculated explicitly, operator action is distinguished from measurement, and checking all basis vectors proves that observables on different factors commute. Product observables are introduced as probes of correlation.

## 01:03:00 to 01:21:25

The antisymmetric singlet becomes the central example. Every local spin average vanishes even though the joint state is pure, which rules out factorization. The discussion covers physical preparation, relaxation, singlet and triplet energies, total spin, entanglement entropy, and the unchanged antisymmetric form in every common spin basis.

## 01:21:25 to 01:34:30

Joint expectation values are evaluated. Equal components are perfectly anticorrelated, mixed orthogonal components have zero correlation, and rotational symmetry packages the result as \(-\delta_{mn}\), or \(-\bm a\cdot\bm b\) for arbitrary detector directions.

## 01:34:30 to 01:42:57

The penny-and-dime analogy separates ordinary prior correlation from what Bell's theorem tests. A classical computer can simulate one spin, but two separated classical machines cannot reproduce every singlet correlation using only local data and shared randomness. This failure of local classical simulation is kept distinct from the ability to transmit a message.

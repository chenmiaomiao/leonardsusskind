# Narrative Map

## 1. Measurement Before Motion

Set spin aside only long enough to collect the measurement principles. Begin with things an apparatus can return, then use up/down and up/right to turn orthogonality into a physical statement.

## 2. Probability Has Structure

Move from possible eigenvalues to their probabilities. Keep amplitudes, absolute squares, normalization, notation, degeneracy, and unknown preparation in one continuous argument rather than presenting Born's rule as an isolated formula.

## 3. Reversibility Becomes Geometry

Use the classical coin and the minus first law to motivate a common linear \(U(t)\). Preserve distinguishability first, then strengthen it to conservation of every overlap. The unitarity equation should feel inevitable at the end of this progression.

## 4. Small Time Reveals the Generator

Approach \(U(\epsilon)\) from the identity, derive \(H^\dagger=H\), and only then name \(H\) as the Hamiltonian. Keep the questions about selection of \(H\), explicit time dependence, and long intervals next to the derivation they qualify.

## 5. From State Motion to Observable Motion

After the Schrodinger equation is established, pause over what an average means. The mean-versus-mode discussion and the sample-average exchange prepare the operator-sandwich formula rather than interrupting it.

## 6. The Classical Shadow

Differentiate the sandwich, introduce the commutator from the order-sensitive algebra, and compare its properties with the Poisson bracket. Quantum mechanics remains primary; the classical equation is the approximation to be recovered.

## 7. A First Conservation Law

Set \(L=H\), use \([H,H]=0\), and claim exactly what follows: average energy is constant when \(H\) has no explicit time dependence. End with both promised continuations, the spin calculation and measurement through entanglement.

# Chapter Plan

## Lecture Arc

The chapter first restores the four measurement principles: observables and Hermitian operators, eigenvalues as outcomes, distinguishability as orthogonality, and Born's rule. Spin preparations give the operational meaning of distinguishability. The discussion then handles eigenvector notation, normalization, degenerate eigenspaces, and unknown preparations through the audience exchanges.

The new material begins with dynamics. Classical reversibility becomes conservation of quantum overlap. A common linear time-development operator is therefore unitary. Expanding it for a small interval produces a Hermitian generator, identifies that generator as the Hamiltonian, and yields the abstract time-dependent Schrodinger equation.

The final third changes from states to observable averages. It distinguishes mean from mode and sample average, proves the operator-sandwich formula, differentiates it, and obtains the commutator equation. Antisymmetry and product rules reveal the Poisson-bracket correspondence. Setting the observable equal to the time-independent Hamiltonian proves conservation of average energy. Measurement collapse remains deferred until system-apparatus entanglement can be discussed.

## Required Mathematical Sequence

1. \(L\ket{\lambda}=\lambda\ket{\lambda}\), with real outcomes and orthogonal distinguishable states.
2. \(P(\lambda)=|\braket{\lambda}{A}|^2\), including normalization and degenerate-channel addition.
3. \(\ket{\psi(t)}=U(t)\ket{\psi(0)}\), with one linear \(U\) for every state.
4. Conservation of arbitrary overlaps and \(U^\dagger U=I\).
5. \(U(\epsilon)=I-i\epsilon H\), first-order unitarity, and \(H^\dagger=H\).
6. \(i\hbar\,\partial_t\ket{\psi}=H\ket{\psi}\), including finite-time composition.
7. \(\expect{L}=\bra{\psi}L\ket{\psi}\).
8. \(d\expect{L}/dt=(i/\hbar)\expect{[H,L]}\).
9. Commutator and Poisson-bracket antisymmetry and product rules.
10. \([A,B]\leftrightarrow i\hbar\{A,B\}_{\mathrm{PB}}\).
11. \(d\expect{H}/dt=0\) for time-independent \(H\).

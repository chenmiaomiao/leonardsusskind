# Narrative Map

## Sequence

### Operational Spin

The spin is defined through preparation and detection. Analyzer orientation matters, every readout is binary, and immediate repetition confirms the newly prepared value. The arrow on the board remains a label until the inversion experiment and ensemble statistics supply operational geometry.

### From Events to Averages

A \(z\)-prepared spin gives random \(x\) and \(y\) results, while an arbitrary \(\hat n\)-preparation measured along \(\hat m\) has mean \(\hat n\cdot\hat m\). The lecture repeatedly contrasts discrete events with classical-looking averages.

### Logic Before Formalism

Classical propositions are subsets, so union and intersection commute. The sequential test of \(A:\sigma_z=+1\) or \(B:\sigma_x=+1\) changes when its order is reversed. Measurement disturbance has entered the logic itself.

### Linear State Space

Complex linear combinations, bras, kets, inner products, and orthogonality provide the replacement structure. A single spin lives in \(\mathbb C^2\), and perfectly distinguishable alternatives are orthogonal.

### Three Basis Pairs

The up--down pair is chosen as a basis. Equal probabilities and orthogonality determine the right--left pair, while a relative imaginary phase determines the in--out pair. Each pair is mutually unbiased with respect to the other two.

### Physical Parameters

Two complex amplitudes contain four real parameters. Unit normalization removes one and common phase removes another, leaving the two real parameters of a spatial direction. This remains a heuristic connection until observables and rotations are formalized.

## Recurring Distinctions

- Spatial direction versus abstract state vector.
- One spin measured repeatedly versus many identical preparations.
- Individual outcome versus ensemble average.
- Spatial perpendicularity versus state-space orthogonality.
- Common phase versus measurable relative phase.
- A fitted state-vector construction versus the operator formalism still to come.

# Editorial Analysis: Lecture 9

## Lecture Arc

The opening closes the previous spin discussion by putting photon number into the same Hilbert-space language as the electron pair. The central mathematical movement then develops plane waves, a reciprocal Fourier pair, and the Dirac delta before returning to the physics of a particle on a line. Position and momentum emerge as alternative continuous bases, their wave functions are Fourier transforms of one another, and their uncertainty is presented as the reciprocal-width property of a Fourier pair.

The final movement turns representation theory into dynamics. The deliberately simple Hamiltonian \(H=cP\) produces rigid translation and is compared directly with Hamilton's equations. Its chiral and negative-energy limitations motivate the ordinary free-particle Hamiltonian \(P^2/2m\), whose nonlinear dispersion makes wave packets spread.

## Physics Corrections And Qualifications

- The delta function and continuum eigenkets are generalized distributions, not square-integrable states.
- Plane-wave normalization is justified by a finite periodic box or distributional limit.
- Position alone labels a complete basis in one dimension; compatible observables from different spatial directions may label a higher-dimensional basis jointly.
- The time-energy relation is not introduced as an operator pair identical to \(X,P\).
- \(H=cP\) is a restricted one-direction model, not the Hamiltonian of an ordinary massive free particle.
- \(H=P^2/2m\) has group velocity \(p/m\), so a finite-width packet disperses.

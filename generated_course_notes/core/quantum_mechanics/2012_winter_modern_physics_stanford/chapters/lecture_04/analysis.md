# Chapter Analysis

## Lecture Arc

The lecture begins by transporting the discrete measurement rules into the
continuum. Probability masses become densities, Kronecker deltas become
Dirac deltas, and a lattice regulator explains the otherwise puzzling
normalization of position states. The position amplitude then gives the Born
density without introducing a new postulate.

A particle on a circle supplies the central example. Periodicity makes the
momentum spectrum discrete and gives orbital angular momentum in integer
units of hbar. The large-radius limit motivates the distinction between a
dense spectrum and a classical state. Position and momentum are then shown
to be incompatible first by their eigenfunctions and then by the canonical
commutator.

The final movement turns this algebra into the two-slit experiment.
Amplitudes add before probabilities are formed, producing the interference
cross term. Questions about fringe spacing, one-aperture diffraction,
particle detections, photons, path detectors, and reversibility carry the
argument to the boundary between kinematics and dynamics.

## Required Classroom Exchanges

- Restating the continuous Born rule.
- Explaining divergent continuum normalization with a lattice.
- Distinguishing a dense momentum spectrum from classical mechanics.
- Explaining why radius cancels from orbital angular momentum.
- Interpreting the canonical commutator and scalar eigenvalues.
- Treating equal local wave numbers, vertical momentum, and energy.
- Reconciling localized detections with a wave-like distribution.
- Comparing electron and photon interference.
- Separating one-aperture diffraction from two-path interference.
- Qualifying exact dark fringes, unequal apertures, and wavelength scales.
- Explaining source preparation, which-path information, reuse, and reversal.

## Mathematical Refinements

- The Dirac delta is treated as a distribution, and unit area is separated
  from Hilbert-space norm.
- Continuum position and momentum kets are generalized, delta-normalized
  eigenstates obtained from explicit lattice rescaling.
- The momentum operator on the circle includes periodic boundary conditions
  in its domain.
- Integer orbital angular momentum is separated from half-integer spin.
- Simultaneous diagonalization is stated for Hermitian operators with the
  needed domain qualification in infinite dimensions.
- The commutator is anti-Hermitian; its Hermitian multiple carries no
  state-dependent observable information.
- Far-field fringe spacing and unequal-amplitude visibility are included to
  make the diffraction claims quantitatively correct.
- Reusing a re-prepared electron is distinguished from reversing a complete
  detection event.

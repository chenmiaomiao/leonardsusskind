# Narrative Map

## 1. The Smallest Useful Quantum System

Freeze the photon's direction and momentum, review the wire-grid polarizer,
and expose the conflict between continuous preparation angles and discrete
detector outcomes.

## 2. The Axial Basis

Introduce horizontal and vertical state vectors, orthogonality, global phase,
and the first two-outcome observable.

## 3. The Diagonal Basis

Rotate by forty-five degrees, distinguish amplitudes from probabilities, and
construct the diagonal observable.

## 4. Arbitrary Linear Polarization

Use rotated unit vectors and trigonometric identities to derive the
single-photon form of Malus's law.

## 5. A Measurement Changes the Route

Explain why a successful intermediate test prepares a new state and turns
zero direct transmission into a nonzero two-stage probability.

## 6. The General Linear Observable

Build the spectral and matrix forms of the analyzer at angle theta, verify its
eigenvector, and interpret a ninety-degree rotation.

## 7. Circular Polarization

Introduce complex basis vectors, quadrature electromagnetic fields,
quarter-wave plates, circular statistics, and the circular observable.

## 8. Compatibility

Use common eigenvectors and commutators to distinguish equivalent orthogonal
axis labels from genuinely incompatible analyzers.

## 9. Expectation as Average

Motivate the terminology with a two-lobe distribution, derive the spectral
average, and evaluate the axial polarization example.

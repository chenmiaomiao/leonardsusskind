# Lecture Analysis

## Scope

Lecture 6 develops photon polarization as the smallest useful quantum system.
It begins with wire-grid preparation and measurement, resolves the tension
between continuous polarization axes and binary outcomes, constructs linear
and circular polarization observables, and closes by deriving expectation
value as a probability-weighted average.

## Chronological Argument

1. A fixed-momentum photon leaves a two-dimensional transverse degree of
   freedom.
2. A polarizer both prepares a state and tests a previously prepared state.
3. Rotational symmetry allows continuously many states although a fixed test
   has only two outcomes.
4. Axial, diagonal, and arbitrary-angle bases make amplitudes and Malus's law
   explicit.
5. An intermediate polarizer changes the state and can open an otherwise
   blocked route.
6. Spectral decomposition constructs the observable for any analyzer angle.
7. Complex amplitudes produce circular and elliptical polarization.
8. Noncommuting polarization observables illustrate incompatibility.
9. Inserting a complete eigenbasis turns the operator sandwich into an
   ordinary weighted average.

## Source Clarifications

- The wire direction is separated from the transmitted electric-field axis.
- A polarization state is a ray, so a common phase has no observable effect.
- The intermediate-polarizer probability is written as a product of
  conditional probabilities.
- Circular-polarization handedness is left convention-dependent.
- The lecture's elliptical-polarization wording is tightened: equal
  magnitudes and relative phase plus or minus pi over two give circular
  polarization; general complex ratios give ellipses.
- The expectation value is distinguished from a likely single-shot result.

Opening institutional material and closing credits are not lecture content.

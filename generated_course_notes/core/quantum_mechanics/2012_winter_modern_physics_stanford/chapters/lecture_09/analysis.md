# Lecture 9 Analysis

## Central Arc

The lecture moves from the historical failure of classical repair strategies
to a quantum-first derivation of free motion and, finally, back to classical
mechanics as an approximation. The mathematical spine is:

1. choose the nonrelativistic free Hamiltonian;
2. derive and solve the free Schrodinger equation;
3. construct arbitrary states by Fourier superposition;
4. compare commutators with Poisson brackets;
5. derive the two Ehrenfest equations.

## Editorial Decisions

- Preserve the historical argument because it establishes why quantum
  mechanics, rather than classical mechanics, is fundamental.
- Preserve all twenty substantive classroom exchanges in focused
  question-and-answer boxes.
- Keep hbar explicit in the Fourier normalization and phases.
- Explain the lecture's positive-time-exponent sign convention before
  returning to the conventional positive-frequency plane wave.
- Add the Jacobian required for a continuum probability density in energy.
- Qualify plane waves as generalized eigenstates and Fourier completeness as
  applying to physically admissible states.
- Distinguish exact Ehrenfest evolution from the narrow-packet approximation
  that closes Newton's equation on the packet center.

# Editorial Analysis

## Lecture Arc

The lecture begins with the classical Hamiltonian as the generator of a
continuous phase-space flow. It then asks for the quantum analogue: a
continuous transformation that preserves inner products. Expanding that
unitary transformation over an infinitesimal time step identifies a Hermitian
generator and leads directly to the abstract Schrodinger equation.

An anisotropic polarization medium makes the formalism observable. Two energy
eigenpolarizations acquire different phases, and a rotated analyzer converts
their relative phase into an oscillating detection probability. The final
movement derives the commutator law for expectation values, identifies
conserved quantities, and applies the same Hamiltonian principle to a free
particle on a line.

## Editorial Decisions

- Preserve the classical review because it establishes the generator analogy
  used throughout the lecture.
- Retain all seven substantive audience exchanges as focused classroom
  questions and answers.
- Keep the polarization experiment in the lecture's order, including the
  physical questions about material extent, propagation distance, circular
  input, and preferred axes.
- Correct the intermediate-polarization description: between the linear
  extrema the state is generally elliptical and is circular at the appropriate
  quarter-cycle points.
- Restore every factor of i and hbar in the free-particle derivation while
  preserving the board correction as a classroom exchange.
- Qualify time-independent-Hamiltonian claims, infinite-dimensional operator
  domains, and ideal plane-wave normalization without interrupting the
  lecture's rhythm.

## Acceptance Basis

All 54 chronological movements are represented. Twenty board frames were
checked against nearby candidates and retained only when they directly support
the neighboring argument. The standalone chapter compiles cleanly and has
been reviewed page by page.

# Narrative Map

## Opening

The lecture returns to the elementary question of change with time. Discrete
classical systems hop between states; continuous systems flow through phase
space, and the Hamiltonian determines that flow.

## Main Movements

1. Review Hamilton's equations and express classical evolution with a Poisson
   bracket.
2. Demand that closed quantum evolution preserve every inner product and
   therefore be unitary.
3. Expand a short time step and show that its generator must be Hermitian.
4. Take the infinitesimal limit to obtain the abstract Schrodinger equation.
5. Let two polarization eigenstates acquire different energy phases in an
   anisotropic medium.
6. Use a rotated analyzer to turn relative phase into observable
   interference.
7. Differentiate an expectation value and derive its commutator evolution
   law.
8. Identify commuting observables as conserved quantities.
9. Represent a particle on a line by a wave function, construct the free
   Hamiltonian, and solve for a momentum eigenstate.

## Expository Rhythm

Each abstraction is followed by a physical or calculational test. Audience
questions remain where they clarify the classical limit, experimental
geometry, polarization behavior, operator ordering, or missing constants.
Board corrections are resolved in the displayed equations without erasing the
reason the correction was needed.

# Chapter Analysis

## Lecture Arc

The lecture begins as a continuation of the linear-algebra construction. It
moves from abstract operators to matrix elements, basis components, and
operator products before asking which operators can represent measurements.
Hermitian operators supply the transition from algebra to physics: their
real spectra and orthonormal eigenbases make it possible to state the five
measurement postulates.

The second half tests those postulates on a particle moving on a line.
Position becomes multiplication by x, its generalized eigenvectors become
Dirac delta distributions, and the wave function becomes the position-basis
component of the state. Differentiation then supplies a second operator.
Integration by parts shows that minus i times the derivative is Hermitian,
its generalized eigenfunctions are plane waves, and de Broglie's relation
identifies its eigenvalue with momentum in units of hbar. The contrast
between localized position states and delocalized momentum states closes the
lecture with the first structural view of uncertainty.

## Required Classroom Exchanges

- Converting a vector between two complete bases and interpreting the two
  matrix indices.
- Whether arbitrary operators possess eigenvectors.
- Whether rotations are Hermitian and which space the eigenvectors span.
- Distinguishing states, observables, eigenvectors, eigenvalues, and real
  experimental error bars.
- The meaning of a definite observable, illustrated by spin along different
  axes.
- Whether function-space inner products are real.
- The convergence and boundary assumptions on wave functions.
- Whether delta-localized position eigenstates are orthonormal.
- Whether the Born rule restricts the wave function to one special shape.
- The conflict between decaying domains and nondecaying plane waves.
- The wavelength relation, the helix visualization, deferred collapse, and
  the later dynamical justification of momentum.

## Mathematical Refinements

- Over a finite-dimensional complex space, a general operator has at least
  one eigenvector but need not have a complete eigenbasis. The Hermitian
  spectral theorem supplies a complete orthonormal eigenbasis.
- A finite rotation is unitary, not generally Hermitian. Its infinitesimal
  generator is anti-Hermitian, or minus i times a Hermitian generator.
- The rectangular delta approximation of width epsilon and height
  one over epsilon has unit area, not unit Hilbert-space norm.
- Position and plane-wave eigenstates belong to a generalized continuous
  spectrum and are delta-normalized rather than ordinary normalized vectors.
- The Born rule for position gives a probability density; probabilities are
  integrals over intervals.
- Hermiticity of the derivative operator depends on its domain and boundary
  conditions.
- The plane waves do not satisfy the decay assumptions used in the
  integration-by-parts proof. They are handled as generalized eigenstates or
  as limits of normalizable packets.
- The identification p equals hbar k is provisional until time evolution and
  the classical wave-packet limit are developed.

element, component action, operator product, Hermitian spectrum, discrete

# Narrative Map

## Opening

Quantum mechanics is linear algebra. The lecture therefore resumes with the
objects already introduced: states as vectors and operators as maps from one
state vector to another. Matrix elements and a basis are introduced not as a
new formalism but as the way to calculate with those abstract objects.

## Sequence

1. An operator acts on a ket, and a bra converts the result into a matrix
   element.
2. A chosen orthonormal basis converts every ket into components and every
   operator into a square matrix.
3. Completeness turns operator action into matrix times column, and a second
   completeness insertion turns operator composition into matrix
   multiplication.
4. Hermitian operators are isolated because measurements return real values.
5. Eigenvectors and eigenvalues lead to the finite-dimensional spectral
   theorem. Audience objections about generic operators and rotations mark
   the boundary of that theorem.
6. The five measurement postulates distinguish states, observables,
   outcomes, definite states, and probabilities.
7. A particle on a line replaces finite columns by complex functions.
8. Multiplication by x is the position operator. Its eigenvalue equation
   forces support at one point and introduces the Dirac delta distribution.
9. Delta sampling identifies the wave function with the component of the
   state in the position basis. The Born rule then becomes a density
   statement.
10. Differentiation is linear but anti-Hermitian. Multiplication by minus i
    produces the Hermitian wave-number operator.
11. Its generalized eigenfunctions are plane waves. Their constant
    magnitude makes them maximally delocalized in position.
12. Wavelength and de Broglie's relation give p equals hbar k.
13. Position and momentum define incompatible generalized bases. A helix
    picture makes plane-wave phase visible, while questions about collapse
    and classical motion point forward to later lectures.

## Voice

The chapter should read as a prepared derivation rather than a report about a
video. Each section begins from the question that motivates the next
calculation. Classroom exchanges remain explicit where they correct a
possible misconception. Mathematical refinements are folded into the
argument at the point where they are needed, without changing the order or
the pedagogical rhythm of the lecture.

## Ending

The lecture does not end with a summary. It ends with open dynamical tasks:
construct the momentum probability distribution, discuss what measurement
does to the state, introduce time evolution, and show that wave packets
recover classical motion.

# Narrative Map

## Opening

The unfinished circular-polarization exercise supplies a concrete entry
point. Once all three polarization observables are on the board, the lecture
widens to ask what states and observables do in classical and quantum
mechanics.

## Main Movements

1. Complete the circular observable and recall the operational meaning of
   orthogonality.
2. Compare finite classical states, continuous phase space, and quantum
   vector states.
3. Isolate expectation values and transition probabilities as the physical
   questions answered by a state.
4. Show that both questions ignore a common phase, so a physical pure state is
   a ray rather than one vector.
5. Count the two physical parameters of a polarization ray and interpret them
   as an ellipse.
6. Use analyzer overlaps to recover orientation and signed ellipticity.
7. Move from state description to time evolution by demanding preservation of
   distinctions and then of every inner product.
8. Develop operator action on bras, adjoints, and Hermitian observables.
9. Derive the unitary condition and end with the unit-modulus eigenvalue
   question that opens the next lecture.

## Expository Rhythm

Concrete polarization examples alternate with abstraction. Audience
questions are retained where they test a parameter count, expose a conjugation
error, distinguish single trials from ensembles, or force a mathematical
qualification. Board self-corrections are resolved in the equations without
removing the question that caused them.

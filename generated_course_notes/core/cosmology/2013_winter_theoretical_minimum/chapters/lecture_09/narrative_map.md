# Narrative Map

## Opening

A student recalls \(\ddot\phi+3H\dot\phi=-V'(\phi)\) and asks what the force acts upon. The answer backs up to first principles: a scalar field has a value at each point, stores potential energy, and can move through field space. This question becomes the lecture rather than interrupting it.

## Beat Sequence

1. Define \(V(\phi)\), distinguish potential from kinetic field energy, use the vibrating-string analogy, and normalize the field coordinate.
2. Derive \(\ddot\phi=-V'(\phi)\) in a fixed box and interpret the force in field space.
3. Restore the physical volume \(a^3\), repeat the variation, and derive Hubble friction.
4. Translate \(3H\dot\phi\) into honey-like drag and terminal motion; answer the least-action and energy-conservation questions.
5. Couple the scalar to Friedmann expansion. A shallow slope keeps \(\phi\) slow while a high potential keeps \(a\) fast.
6. Motivate inflation through monopole dilution, smoothness, flatness, and the approximate sixty-\(e\)-fold lower bound.
7. Leave the plateau, reheat the universe, and distinguish the beginning of the hot Big Bang from the beginning of space.
8. Test the potential picture through classroom questions about curvature, monopoles, vacuum energy, reheating, causality, the field-space axis, and unknown microphysics.
9. Follow a persistent vacuum energy into the far future, retaining the questions about horizons, redshifted broadcasts, bound galaxies, probes, and lost observational access.
10. Return to structure. Compare the real sky, simulations, and swimming-pool caustics; derive the one-dimensional Jacobian and extend the geometry to sheets, filaments, and knots.
11. End with the unresolved seed: the initial velocity variations are themselves inhomogeneities whose quantum origin remains to be explained.

## Pacing Rules

- Keep the fixed-box and expanding-box derivations separate.
- Let the friction analogy precede the Friedmann feedback.
- Do not move the monopole and smoothness motivations ahead of the equations.
- Preserve the reheating and far-future questions as part of the argument, not as a disposable appendix.
- Keep explicit ignorance where the potential shape, initial state, inflaton identity, and vacuum hierarchy are unknown.
- Treat caustics as the bridge to primordial quantum fluctuations, not as a detached mathematical exercise.

# Lecture 10 Narrative Map

## Formal Lecture

1. Begin with boiling water but choose the Ising route rather than van der Waals.
2. Rebuild link energy, field energy, broken-bond cost, and lattice counting.
3. Replace \(2d\) neighbors by their average and derive the self-consistency equation.
4. Read the critical temperature from line--tanh tangency.
5. Use a tiny field to select a branch and distinguish a fixed-temperature jump from a continuous route around the endpoint.
6. Make chemical potential concrete with a permeable potential-well box and the grand partition sum.
7. Interpret down spin as empty and up spin as occupied.
8. Count \(8J,16J,12J\) to expose hard-core exclusion and a \(4J\) attraction.
9. Derive the exact Ising--lattice-gas dictionary and \(\rho=(1+m)/2\).
10. Translate the coexistence line into boiling, then close with critical exponents, universality, interfaces, latent heat, and detector sensitivity.

## Postlecture Discussion

11. Separate smallness from fine-tuning through the blimp and submarine analogies.
12. Introduce many realized domains plus conditional observation, then apply the logic cautiously to vacuum energy.
13. Discuss correlated parameter bands, landscape proposals, symmetry, and the limits of selection without a mechanism.
14. Preserve exchanges on uncertainty, nuclear and atomic sensitivity, inflationary disagreement, time-varying dark energy, causal order, and testability.

## Rhythm

The chapter keeps the lecture's backward steps before each conceptual pivot: first rebuild the magnet, then define chemical potential before revealing the lattice gas, then count bonds before presenting the algebraic dictionary. Classroom objections remain at the points where they interrupt and sharpen the argument. The postlecture material begins only after the formal course closing.

## Pacing Risks Avoided

- Do not compress the graphical branch argument into a formula.
- Do not call \(h\) directly equal to \(\mu\); retain the exchange offset.
- Do not erase the live \(4J\) correction.
- Do not turn metastability, hysteresis, pressure, or latent heat into calculations absent from the lecture.
- Do not present anthropic selection as a dynamical mechanism or established landscape theory.

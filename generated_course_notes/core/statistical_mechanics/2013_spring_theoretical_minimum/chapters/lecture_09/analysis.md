# Lecture 9 Analysis

## Expository Arc

The chapter follows the lecture's actual order rather than reorganizing it as a reference article. It first corrects the Ising history, rebuilds the one-spin canonical calculation, and asks whether an interacting chain can retain information over an arbitrary distance. Bond variables then give the exact one-dimensional partition function and correlation decay. The telephone analogy motivates the move to many neighbors, where mean field becomes a self-consistency problem. The graphical transition is followed by the lecture's two objections: domain-wall entropy defeats mean field in one dimension, and an infinitesimal field is needed to select an ordered branch. The geomagnetic-reversal exchange remains as a final, explicitly bounded contrast.

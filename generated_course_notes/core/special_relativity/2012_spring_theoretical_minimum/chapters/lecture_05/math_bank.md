# Math Bank: Lecture 5

## Doppler Geometry

- Successive right-moving crests: \(x=t\) and \(x=t+1\).
- Primed simultaneity: \(t'=0\), hence \(t=vx\).
- Intersection: \(x=1/(1-v)\) and \(t=v/(1-v)\).
- Wavelength factor: \(\lambda'/\lambda=\sqrt{(1+\beta)/(1-\beta)}=\sqrt{(c+v)/(c-v)}\).
- Opposite direction: replace \(v\) by \(-v\); the frequency factor is reciprocal.

## Coupled Particle And Field

- Separable mechanics: \(L=L_x(x,\dot x)+L_y(y,\dot y)\).
- Mixed interaction prototype: \(L\supset\kappa xy\).
- Particle coupling: \(L_{\mathrm p}=-(m+g\phi)\sqrt{1-\dot x^2}\).
- Restored units: \(L_{\mathrm p}=-(mc^2+g\phi)\sqrt{1-\dot x^2/c^2}\).
- Slow limit: \(L_{\mathrm{NR}}=\frac12m\dot x^2-g\phi\), so \(V=g\phi\).
- Worldline interaction: \(S_{\mathrm{int}}=-g\int dt\,\phi(\mathbf 0,t)\).
- Delta identity: \(\int d^3x\,\phi(\mathbf x,t)\delta^3(\mathbf x)=\phi(\mathbf 0,t)\).
- Sourced wave equation: \(\partial_t^2\phi-\nabla^2\phi=-g\delta^3(\mathbf x)\).
- Static limit: \(\nabla^2\phi=g\delta^3(\mathbf x)\).
- Moving source: \(\delta^3(\mathbf x-\mathbf a(t))\).

## Relativistic Notation

- Coordinates: \(x^\mu=(t,x,y,z)\).
- Metric: \(\eta_{\mu\nu}=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)\).
- Lowering: \(A_\mu=\eta_{\mu\nu}A^\nu\).
- Contraction: \(A^\mu A_\mu=-(A^0)^2+(A^1)^2+(A^2)^2+(A^3)^2\).
- Scalar differential: \(d\phi=\partial_\mu\phi\,dx^\mu\).
- Raised gradient: \(\partial^\mu\phi=\eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_\nu\phi\).
- Free density: \(\mathcal L_0=-\frac12\partial_\mu\phi\,\partial^\mu\phi\).
- Galilean limit: \(c\to\infty\) gives \(x'=x-vt\) and \(t'=t\).

## Klein--Gordon Modes And Mass Shift

- Density: \(\mathcal L=\frac12[(\partial_t\phi)^2-|\nabla\phi|^2-\mu^2\phi^2]\).
- Equation: \(\partial_t^2\phi-\nabla^2\phi+\mu^2\phi=0\).
- Plane wave: \(\phi=e^{-i\omega t+i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}=e^{ik_\mu x^\mu}\).
- Dispersion: \(\omega^2=|\mathbf k|^2+\mu^2\).
- Particle relations: \(E=\hbar\omega\), \(\mathbf p=\hbar\mathbf k\), and \(\mu=mc/\hbar\).
- Scalar background: \(m_{\mathrm{eff}}=m+g\phi_0\).
- Atomic scale: \(a_0\sim1/(\alpha m_e)\) in natural units.

## Editorial Clarifications

- The coupling sign may be absorbed into g, but one convention must be maintained through both equations of motion.
- The scalar field has mass dimension one only in the stated four-dimensional natural-unit normalization.
- Klein--Gordon is used here as a classical scalar field equation, not as a positive-probability one-particle wave equation.
- The Higgs comparison is a toy worldline analogy; fermion and gauge-boson masses arise through different Standard Model couplings.
- Proton and neutron masses are predominantly QCD energy, not direct Higgs mass terms.

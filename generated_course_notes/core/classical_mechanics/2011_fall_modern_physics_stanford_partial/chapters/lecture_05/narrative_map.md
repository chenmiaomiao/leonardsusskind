# Narrative Map

## Opening

The lecture begins with a methodological claim: velocities are usually easier
to calculate than accelerations in generalized coordinates. The examples are
therefore arranged to demonstrate a reliable procedure rather than increasing
physical difficulty monotonically.

## Movement

1. **Establish the method.** Compute coordinates and velocities, form
   \(T-U\), and let the Euler--Lagrange equations organize the dynamics.
2. **Build the simple pendulum.** Geometry fixes the velocity and gravitational
   signs before any equation of motion is attempted.
3. **Read motion from energy.** The \(2mgr\) barrier separates oscillation,
   rotation, and the fine-tuned separatrix.
4. **Attempt the complicated system.** The double pendulum introduces a mixed
   velocity term but no new principle.
5. **Extract structure from complexity.** Removing gravity reveals common
   rotation symmetry, mixed canonical momenta, and a conserved total angular
   momentum.
6. **Return to a universal simple system.** Expanding the pendulum potential
   near its minimum leads naturally to the harmonic oscillator.
7. **Explain the approximation.** Taylor expansion, experimental scale, energy
   conservation, and the inverted potential delimit the small-oscillation
   regime.
8. **Solve the oscillator.** Frequency, two integration constants, amplitude,
   and phase are introduced in the same order as the lecture.
9. **Enter Hamiltonian mechanics.** The oscillator is rewritten in \(x,p\),
   drawn as phase-space ellipses, and used to preview area preservation and
   Legendre-transform regularity.

## Rhythm To Preserve

- Concrete construction before abstract interpretation.
- Questions at sign, coefficient, coordinate, and approximation transitions.
- Blackboard evidence adjacent to the mathematics it verifies.
- Corrections shown as settled reasoning rather than erased from the story.
- Phase space treated as the destination of the lecture, not an unrelated
  appendix.

# Chapter Plan

## Lecture Arc

The lecture begins with the initial-data question left by Lecture 1. A deliberately false first-order force law shows how position alone would determine every time derivative; Newton's second-order law then exposes the independent role of velocity. Energy and momentum are reviewed next, with explicit warnings that their conservation is deeper than the elementary Newtonian proofs. The second half changes scale: stationary points of ordinary functions lead to functionals of complete paths, shortest-distance and least-time examples motivate the calculus of variations, and the action is finally revealed as the integral of \(T-U\).

6. Arc length, functionals, Fermat's principle, and the hanging chain.
7. The action, the Lagrangian, equivalent formulations, and the deferred Euler--Lagrange problem.

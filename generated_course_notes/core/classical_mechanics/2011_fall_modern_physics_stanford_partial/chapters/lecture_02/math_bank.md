# Mathematical Content

## Transcript-Backed Equations

- False first-order law: \(F(x)=m\dot x\).
- Higher derivatives: \(m\ddot x=F'(x)\dot x\) and \(m\dddot x=F''(x)\dot x^2+F'(x)\ddot x\).
- Newtonian law and initial data: \(F(x)=m\ddot x\), with \(x(t_0)\) and \(\dot x(t_0)\).
- Conservative force: \(F_i=-\partial U/\partial x_i\).
- Energy: \(T=\frac12m\sum_i v_i^2\), \(E=T+U\), and \(\dot E=\sum_i v_i(F_i-F_i)=0\).
- Momentum: \(\mathbf p=m\mathbf v\), \(\mathbf F=\dot{\mathbf p}\), and \(\dot{\mathbf P}=0\) for a closed system.
- Stationarity: \(df/dy=0\) and \(\partial f/\partial x_i=0\).
- Quadratic example: \(f=x^2+y^2+xy+3x+2y+7\).
- Arc length: \(\mathcal S[y]=\int\sqrt{1+(y')^2}\,dx\).
- Least time: \(\mathcal T[y]=\int\sqrt{1+(y')^2}/c(y)\,dx\).
- Action: \(A[q]=\int L(q,\dot q)\,dt\), with \(L=T-U\) for Newtonian particles.

## Standard Clarifications

- The displayed quadratic equations give \(x=-4/3,\ y=-1/3\); the spoken and written \(x=-8/3\) is an arithmetic slip.
- Fixed endpoint data can admit multiple classical paths; each stationary path satisfies the same local equations.
- The hanging-chain curve \(y=a\cosh((x-x_0)/a)+y_0\) is supplied as standard context, not as a derivation claimed by the lecture.
- Relativistic energy and momentum are described as one energy--momentum structure without deriving four-vector notation.

## Cautions

- The false law is pedagogical, not physically or dimensionally complete.
- The magnetic-force exchange is exploratory and should remain qualified.
- Fermat's principle is stated using the lecture's "least time" language while the chapter's general stationarity caveat remains active.

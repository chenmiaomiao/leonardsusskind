# Narrative Map

## Opening

The announced goal is energy conservation, but the lecture first returns to
the central-force example. This is not a detour: the corrected radial equation
and the cyclic angle provide a concrete instance of the symmetry-conservation
principle before the general theorem.

## Movement

1. **Repair the concrete mechanics.** Polar coordinates produce the
   centrifugal contribution from the kinetic term and angular momentum from
   the absent angle.
2. **Extract the physical picture.** Fixed angular momentum creates an
   inverse-cube outward term, while Cartesian inertia explains its origin.
3. **Prepare the language.** Variations and stationarity turn a verbal
   symmetry statement into an infinitesimal calculation.
4. **Prove the theorem.** A symmetry transforms a classical trajectory and
   moves its endpoints; the equations of motion remove the bulk variation and
   leave a conserved endpoint quantity.
5. **Test familiar symmetries.** Translation gives total momentum and rotation
   gives angular momentum.
6. **Confront the difficult case.** Time translation changes both the sampled
   configuration and the temporal endpoints. The minus sign and two overhangs
   are treated explicitly.
7. **Reach the promised result.** The conserved expression is negated by
   convention to define \(H\), which becomes \(T+U\) for the elementary
   Lagrangian.
8. **Close conceptually.** Regularity, experimental origin, explicit time
   dependence, and the Hamiltonian outlook place the derivation in a wider
   framework.

## Rhythm To Preserve

- Concrete example before abstract theorem.
- Questions at the exact points where the coordinate or sign interpretation
  becomes difficult.
- A theorem followed immediately by familiar checks.
- The Hamiltonian sign flip shown rather than silently normalized.
- The final historical discussion treated as perspective, not a second topic.

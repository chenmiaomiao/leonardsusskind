# Narrative Map

## Beat Sequence

1. Prescribed static fields isolate one charged particle as the dynamical system.
2. Newtonian, action, Lagrangian, and Hamiltonian descriptions are compared.
3. Magnetic force bends motion without changing kinetic energy.
4. Scalar and vector potentials lead to the electromagnetic action.
5. Gauge transformations alter the description by an endpoint term.
6. Canonical momentum differs from mechanical momentum.
7. The Euler--Lagrange equation combines vector-potential derivatives into B.
8. Two gauges reveal complementary translation symmetries.
9. The resulting first-order relations identify cyclotron motion and q over m.
10. Nonzero conserved canonical momenta identify the guiding center.
11. An electric field creates a transverse E cross B drift.
12. The charged-particle Hamiltonian completes the canonical picture.
13. An arbitrary phase-space function leads to the Poisson bracket.
14. Fundamental brackets, antisymmetry, linearity, and Leibniz rule form an algebra.
15. Hamiltonian evolution in bracket form points toward quantum mechanics.

## Pacing Requirements

- Keep the hierarchy of mechanics before the electromagnetic derivation.
- Introduce the ambiguity of A before calling it gauge invariance.
- Derive one Lorentz-force component in full.
- Fix the magnetic-field orientation before comparing gauges.
- Keep the q over m historical remark beside cyclotron motion.
- Place the Hall-device exchange after the drift calculation.
- Derive Poisson evolution before listing algebraic rules.
- End with the quantum-mechanical motivation, not a detached recap.

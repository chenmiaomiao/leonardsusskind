# Narrative Map

## Beat Sequence

1. A one-to-one microscopic law motivates information conservation.
2. Uniform phase-space dust strengthens reversibility into volume
   preservation.
3. Topology, shear, chaos, distance, and friction test the geometric claim.
4. One- and two-dimensional fluid fluxes define zero divergence.
5. Hamilton's crossed derivatives prove Liouville's theorem.
6. A free-particle rescaling shows that canonical area, not either axis, is
   invariant.
7. Long-time probability, chaotic filamentation, coarse-graining, and entropy
   separate exact information from measurable information.
8. The magnetic Lorentz force supplies a fundamental velocity-dependent
   example.
9. The vector potential enters through a path-dependent action and shifts
   canonical momentum away from mechanical momentum.
10. The Euler--Lagrange equation recovers the Lorentz force component by
    component.
11. The magnetic coupling cancels from energy in velocity variables and
    reappears through \(\bm p-q\bm A\) in canonical variables.
12. Hamilton's equations reproduce the same force, with radiation explicitly
    outside the external-field approximation.

## Pacing Requirements

- Keep the friction discussion before the divergence proof; it establishes
  why the full system matters.
- Derive fluid divergence before inserting Hamilton's equations.
- Keep canonical scaling before probability and coarse-graining.
- Treat the magnetic half as a fresh construction, beginning with the force
  and ending with the Hamiltonian check.
- Place every classroom exchange beside the concept that provokes it.
- End with equivalence of Newtonian, Lagrangian, and Hamiltonian descriptions,
  not with a detached summary.

# Narrative Map

## Beat Sequence

1. Two-state update rules establish what it means to run a law both forward
   and backward.
2. Larger finite state spaces split into cycles and conserved sectors.
3. A many-to-one map loses histories; phase-space dust introduces the
   information-preserving flow picture.
4. Position alone is shown to be an incomplete state, forcing the passage
   from configuration space to \((q,p)\).
5. Newton's second-order equations are rewritten as twice as many first-order
   equations without changing the physics.
6. A one-variable Legendre transform is built first from inverse derivatives,
   then from complementary areas, and finally by variation.
7. The transform is extended to many variables, with coordinates held fixed
   while velocities are replaced by momenta.
8. Variation of \(H=\sum_i p_iv_i-L\) and the Euler--Lagrange equations produce
   Hamilton's canonical pair.
9. Questions about invertibility, friction, fields, and hidden degrees of
   freedom delimit the ordinary construction.
10. The particle Hamiltonian verifies the new equations against Newtonian
    mechanics.
11. Pairwise cancellation proves energy conservation and constant-energy
    contours distinguish bounded from running motion.
12. A nonlinear Hamiltonian separates canonical momentum from the special
    relation \(p=m\dot q\), followed by the thermodynamic limit on usable
    energy.
13. The Poisson bracket packages all canonical evolution, and explicit time
    dependence supplies the final refinement.

## Pacing Requirements

- Do not skip the discrete-map opening; it motivates the entire phase-space
  language.
- Keep the area picture before the algebraic Legendre proof.
- Keep questions beside the concept that provokes them rather than collecting
  them at the end.
- Repeat the key cancellation in enough detail for Hamilton's equations to
  feel derived, not announced.
- Preserve the geometric energy-contour payoff before introducing the Poisson
  bracket.
- End on the unified canonical structure rather than an artificial summary
  list.

## Recurring Motifs

- A state must contain enough information to determine a trajectory.
- Hamiltonian mechanics is a reformulation of the same classical physics.
- Conjugate variables appear in reciprocal derivative relations.
- Crossed derivatives create both canonical motion and phase-volume
  preservation.
- Conservation appears first as disconnected sectors, then energy contours,
  and finally a vanishing Poisson bracket.
